Abstract [body]
Empirically, large-scale deep learning models often satisfy a neural scaling law: the test error of the trained model improves polynomially as the model size and data size grow. However, conventional wisdom suggests the test error consists of approximation, bias, and variance errors, where the variance error increases with model size. This disagrees with the general form of neural scaling laws, which predict that increasing model size monotonically improves performance.

We study the theory of scaling laws in an infinite dimensional linear regression setup. Specifically, we consider a model with $M$ parameters as a linear function of sketched covariates. The model is trained by one-pass stochastic gradient descent (SGD) using $N$ data. Assuming the optimal parameter satisfies a Gaussian prior and the data covariance matrix has a power-law spectrum of degree $a>1$, we show that the reducible part of the test error is $\Theta(M^{-(a-1)}+N^{-(a-1)/a})$. The variance error, which increases with $M$, is dominated by the other errors due to the implicit regularization of SGD, thus disappearing from the bound. Our theory is consistent with the empirical neural scaling laws and verified by numerical simulation.

1 Introduction [body]
Deep learning models, particularly those on a large scale, are pivotal in advancing the state-of-the-art across various fields. Recent empirical studies have shed light on the so-called neural scaling laws (see Kaplan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022, for example), which suggest that the generalization performance of these models improves polynomially as both model size, denoted by $M$, and data size, denoted by $N$, increase. The neural scaling law quantitatively describes the population risk as:

\mathcal{R}(M,N)\approx\mathcal{R}^{*}+\frac{c_{1}}{M^{a_{1}}}+\frac{c_{2}}{N^{a_{2}}},

where $\mathcal{R}^{*}$ is a positive irreducible risk and $c_{1}$, $c_{2}$, $a_{1}$, $a_{2}$ are positive constants independent of $M$ and $N$. For instance, by fitting the above formula with empirical measurements in standard large-scale language benchmarks, Hoffmann et al. (2022) estimated $a_{1}\approx 0.34$ and $a_{2}\approx 0.28$, while Besiroglu et al. (2024) estimated that $a_{1}\approx 0.35$ and $a_{2}\approx 0.37$. Though the exact exponents depend on the tasks, neural scaling laws in Equation 1 are observed consistently in practice and are used as principled guidance to build state-of-the-art models, especially under a compute budget (Hoffmann et al., 2022).

From the perspective of statistical learning theory, Equation 1 is rather intriguing. Standard statistical learning bounds (see Mohri et al., 2018; Wainwright, 2019, for example) often decompose the population risk into the sum of irreducible error, approximation error, bias error, and variance error (some theory replaces bias and variance errors by optimization and generalization errors, respectively) as in the form of

\mathcal{R}(M,N)={\mathcal{R}^{*}}+\underbrace{\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{1}{M^{a_{1}}}\bigg)}_{\text{approximation}}+\underbrace{\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{1}{N^{a_{2}}}\bigg)}_{\text{bias}}+\underbrace{\mathcal{O}\bigg(\frac{c(M)}{N^{a_{3}}}\bigg)}_{\text{variance}},

where $a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}$ are positive constants and $c(M)$ is a measure of model complexity that typically increases with the model size $M$. In Equation 2, the approximation error is induced by the mismatch of the best-in-class predictor and the best possible predictor, hence decreasing with the model size $M$. The bias error is induced by the mismatch of the expected algorithm output and the best-in-class predictor, hence decreasing with the data size $N$. The variance error measures the uncertainty of the algorithm output, which decreases with the data size $N$ but increases with the model size $M$ (since the model complexity $c(M)$ increases).

A mystery.

The empirical neural scaling law Equation 1 is incompatible with the typical statistical learning theory bound Equation 2. While the two error terms in the neural scaling law Equation 1 can be explained by the approximation and bias errors in the theoretical bound Equation 2 respectively, it is not clear why the variance error is unobservable when fitting the neural scaling law empirically. This difference must be reconciled, otherwise, the statistical learning theory and the empirical scaling law make conflict predictions: as the model size $M$ increases, the theoretical bound Equation 2 predicts an increase of variance error that eventually causes an increase of the population risk, but the neural scaling law Equation 1 predicts a decrease of the population risk. In other words, it remains unclear when to follow the prediction of the empirical scaling law Equation 1 and when to follow that of the statistical learning bound Equation 2.

Certain prior works provided risk upper bounds that do not grow with model size (see for example (Rudi and Rosasco, 2017; Carratino et al., 2018)). Still, their results are insufficient for studying scaling law as those bounds require a large model size such that the approximation error is ignorable. Moreover, they do not provide instance-wise matching lower bounds to verify the tightness of the upper bounds. See a detailed discussion in Section 2.

Our explanation.

We investigate this issue in an infinite dimensional linear regression setup. We only assume access to $M$-dimensional sketched covariates given by a fixed Gaussian sketch and their responses. We consider a linear predictor with $M$ trainable parameters, which is trained by one-pass stochastic gradient descent (SGD) with geometrically decaying stepsizes using $N$ sketched data. Assuming that the spectrum of the data covariance matrix satisfies a power-law of degree $a>1$ and that the optimal model parameters satisfy a Gaussian prior, we derive matching upper and lower bounds on the population risk achieved by the SGD output (see Theorem 4.1). Specifically, we show that

\mathcal{R}(M,N)=\mathcal{R}^{*}+\underbrace{\Theta\bigg(\frac{1}{M^{a-1}}\bigg)+\tilde{\Theta}\bigg(\frac{1}{(N\gamma)^{(a-1)/a}}\bigg)}_{\text{leading order given by the sum of $\mathsf{Approx}$ and $\mathsf{Bias}$}},\quad\mathsf{Var}=\underbrace{\tilde{\Theta}\bigg(\frac{\min\{M,(N\gamma)^{1/a}\}}{N}\bigg)}_{\text{higher order, thus unobservable}},

where $\gamma=\mathcal{O}(1)$ is the initial stepsize used in SGD and $\tilde{\Theta}(\cdot)$ hides $\log(N)$ factors. In our bound, the sum of the approximation and bias errors determines the order of the excess risk, while the variance error is of a strictly higher order and is therefore nearly unobservable when fitting $\mathcal{R}(M,N)$ as a function of $M$ and $N$ empirically. In addition, our analysis reveals that the small variance error is due to the implicit regularization effect of one-pass SGD (Zou et al., 2021). Our theory suggests that the empirical neural scaling law Equation 1 is a simplification of the statistical learning bound Equation 2 in a special regime when strong regularization (either implicit or explicit) is employed.

Moreover, we generalize the above scaling law to (1) constant stepsize SGD with iterate average (see Theorem F.6), (2) cases where the optimal model parameter satisfies an anisotropic prior (see Theorem 4.2), and (3) where the spectrum of the data covariance matrix satisfies a logarithmic power law (see Theorem 4.3).

Emprical evidence.

Based on our theoretical results, we conjecture that the clean neural scaling law Equation 1 observed in practice is due to the disappearance of variance error caused by strong regularization. Two pieces of empirical evidence to support our understanding. First, large language models that follow the scaling law Equation 1 are often underfitted, as the models are trained over a single pass or a few passes over the data (Komatsuzaki, 2019; Muennighoff et al., 2023; Brown et al., 2020; Touvron et al., 2023). When models are underfitted, the variance error tends to be smaller. Second, when language models are trained with multiple passes (up to $7$ passes), Muennighoff et al. (2023) found that the clean scaling law in Equation 1 no longer holds and they proposed a more sophisticated scaling law to explain their data. This can be explained by a relatively large variance error caused by multiple passes.

We write $f(x)\eqsim g(x)$ (and $f(x)=\Theta(g(x))$) if $f(x)\lesssim g(x)\lesssim f(x)$. For two matrices $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ of appropriate dimensions, we define their inner product by$\langle\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}\rangle:=\tr(\mathbf{A}^{\top}\mathbf{B})$. We use $\|\cdot\|$ to denote the operator norm for matrices and $\ell_{2}$-norm for vectors. Finally, $\log(\cdot)$ refers to logarithm base $2$.

2 Related work [body]
Empirical scaling laws.

In recent years, the scaling laws of deep neural networks in compute, sample size, and model size have been widely studied across different models and domains (Hestness et al., 2017; Rosenfeld et al., 2019; Kaplan et al., 2020; Henighan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022; Zhai et al., 2022; Muennighoff et al., 2023). The early work by Kaplan et al. (2020) first proposed the neural scaling laws of transformer-based models. They observed that the test loss exhibits a power-law decay in quantities including the amount of compute, sample size, and model size, and provided joint formulas in these quantities to predict the test loss. The proposed formulas were later generalized and refined in subsequent works (Henighan et al., 2020; Hoffmann et al., 2022; Alabdulmohsin et al., 2022; Caballero et al., 2022; Muennighoff et al., 2023). Notably, Hoffmann et al. (2022) proposed the Chinchilla law, that is, Equation 1 with $a_{1}\approx 0.34$ and $a_{2}\approx 0.28$. The empirical observation guided them to allocate data and model size under a given compute budget. The Chinchilla law is further revised by Besiroglu et al. (2024). Motivated by the Chinchilla law, Muennighoff et al. (2023) considered the effect of multiple passes over training data and empirically fitted a more sophisticated scaling law that takes account of the effect of data reusing.

Theory of scaling laws.

Although neural scaling laws have been empirically observed over a broad spectrum of problems, there is a relatively limited literature on understanding these scaling laws from a theoretical perspective (Sharma and Kaplan, 2020; Bahri et al., 2021; Maloney et al., 2022; Hutter, 2021; Wei et al., 2022; Michaud et al., 2024; Jain et al., 2024; Bordelon et al., 2024; Atanasov et al., 2024; Nam et al., 2024; Dohmatob et al., 2024). Among these works, Sharma and Kaplan (2020) showed that the test loss scales as $N^{4/d}$ for regression on data with intrinsic dimension $d$. Hutter (2021) studied a toy problem under which a non-trivial power of $N$ arises in the test loss. Jain et al. (2024) considered scaling laws in data selection. Bahri et al. (2021) considered a linear teacher-student model under a power-law spectrum assumption on the covariates, and they showed that the test loss of the ordinary least square estimator decreases following a power law in sample size $N$ (resp. model size $M$) when the model size $M$ (resp. sample size $N$) is infinite. Bordelon et al. (2024) considered a linear random feature model and analyzed the test loss of the solution found by (batch) gradient flow. They focused on the bottleneck regimes where two of the quantities $N,M$, $T$ (training steps) are infinite and showed that the risk has a power-law decay in the remaining quantity. The problem in Bahri et al. (2021); Bordelon et al. (2024) can be viewed as a sketched linear regression model similar to ours. It should be noted that both Bahri et al. (2021) and Bordelon et al. (2024) only derived the dependence of population risk on one of the data size, model size, or training steps in the asymptotic regime where the remaining quantities go to infinity, and their derivations are based on statistical physics heuristics. In comparison, we prove matching (ignoring constant factors) upper and lower risk bounds jointly depending on the finite model size $M$ and data size $N$.

Implicit regularization of SGD.

One-pass SGD in linear regression has been extensively studied in both the classical finite-dimensional setting (Polyak and Juditsky, 1992; Bach and Moulines, 2013; Défossez and Bach, 2015; Dieuleveut et al., 2017; Jain et al., 2018; Jain et al., 2017; Ge et al., 2019) and the modern high-dimensional setting (Dieuleveut and Bach, 2015; Berthier et al., 2020; Zou et al., 2023; Zou et al., 2021; Wu et al., 2022a; Wu et al., 2022b; Varre et al., 2021). In particular, Zou et al. (2021) showed that SGD induces an implicit regularization effect that is comparable to, and in certain cases even more preferable than, the explicit regularization effect induced by ridge regression. This is one of the key motivations of our scaling law interpretation. From a technical perspective, we utilize the sharp finite-sample and dimension-free analysis of SGD developed by Zou et al. (2023); Wu et al. (2022a); Wu et al. (2022b). Different from them, we consider a sequence of linear regression models with an increasing number of trainable parameters given by data sketch. Our main technical innovation is to sharply control the effect of data sketch. Some of our intermediate results, for example, tight bounds on the spectrum of the sketched data covariance under the power law (see Lemma 6.2), might be of independent interest.

Prior works investigated linear regression with random features (Rudi and Rosasco, 2017; Carratino et al., 2018), which can be viewed as a kind of sketched features via random coordinate selection. They mainly focused on the small approximation error regime, where the model size (or the number of features) is much larger than the data size. In comparison, we treat both model size and data size as free variables. Moreover, we provide matching upper and lower bounds while prior works mainly focused on upper bounds. These two innovations are crucial for studying scaling laws that predict test error as a function of both model size and data size. Finally, in the comparable regimes with small or zero approximation error, our excess risk bounds recover the bounds in prior works (Rudi and Rosasco, 2017; Carratino et al., 2018; Pillaud-Vivien et al., 2018; Dieuleveut and Bach, 2015; Defilippis et al., 2024).

3 Setup [body]
We denote a feature vector by $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{H}$, where $\mathbb{H}$ is a Hilbert space that is either finite-dimensional or countably infinite-dimensional, with dimension $d:=\dim(\mathbb{H})$. The corresponding label is denoted by $y\in\mathbb{R}$. In linear regression, we measure the population risk of a parameter $\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{H}$ by the mean squared error,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}) \displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}\rangle-y\big)^{2},\quad\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{H},

where the expectation is over $(\mathbf{x},y)\sim{P}$ for some distribution ${P}$ on $\mathbb{H}\times{\mathbb{R}}$.

Definition 1 (Data covariance and optimal parameter).

Let $\mathbf{H}:=\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{\top}]$ be the data covariance. Assume that $\tr(\mathbf{H})$ and all entries of $\mathbf{H}$ are finite. Let $(\lambda_{i})_{i\geq 0}$ be the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ sorted in non-increasing order. Let $\mathbf{w}^{*}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{w}}\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w})$ be the optimal model parameter. Assume that $\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}:=(\mathbf{w}^{*})^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{w}^{*}$ is finite.

Footnote: 1 1 If $\arg\min\mathcal{R}(\cdot)$ is not unique, we choose $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ to be the minimizer with minimal $\mathbf{H}$-norm.

We only assume access to $M$-dimensional sketched covariates and their responses, that is, $(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x},y)$, where $\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\times\mathbb{H}$ is a fixed sketch matrix. We focus on the Gaussian sketch matrix, that is, entries of $\mathbf{S}$ are independently sampled from $\mathcal{N}\big(0,1/M\big).$ We then consider linear predictors with $M$ trainable parameters given by

Footnote: 2 2 Our results can be extended to other sketching methods (see Woodruff et al., 2014, for example).

f_{\mathbf{v}}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\mathbf{x}\mapsto\langle\mathbf{v},\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}\rangle,

where $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}$ are the trainable parameters. Varying $M$ should be viewed as a linear analog of varying the neural network model size. Our sketched linear regression setting is comparable to the teacher-student setting considered by Bahri et al. (2021); Bordelon et al. (2024).

We consider the training of $f_{\mathbf{v}}$ via one-pass stochastic gradient descent (SGD), that is,

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}_{t} \displaystyle:=\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-\gamma_{t}\big(f_{\mathbf{v}_{t-1}}(\mathbf{x}_{t})-y_{t}\big)\nabla_{\mathbf{v}}f_{\mathbf{v}_{t-1}}(\mathbf{x}_{t}) \displaystyle:=\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-\gamma_{t}\big(\mathbf{x}_{t}^{\top}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}_{t-1}-y_{t}\big)\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}_{t}, \displaystyle t=1,\dots,N,

where $(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t})_{t=1}^{N}$ are independent samples from ${P}$ and $(\gamma_{t})_{t=1}^{N}$ are the stepsizes. We consider a popular geometric decaying stepsize scheduler (Ge et al., 2019; Wu et al., 2022a),

\text{for $t=1,\dots,N$},\ \gamma_{t}:=\gamma/2^{\ell},\ \text{where}\ \ell=\lfloor t/(N/\log(N))\rfloor.

Here, the initial stepsize $\gamma$ is a hyperparameter for the SGD algorithm. Without loss of generality, we assume the initial parameter is $\mathbf{v}_{0}=0$. The output of the SGD algorithm is the last iterate $\mathbf{v}_{N}$. Our proof techniques apply to other stepsize schedulers (e.g., polynomial decay) as well, but we focus on geometric decay as it is known to achieve near minimax-optimal excess risk for the last iterate of SGD (Ge et al., 2019).

Conditioning on a sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}\times\mathbb{H}$, each parameter $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}$ induces a sketched predictor through $\mathbf{x}\mapsto\langle\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v},\mathbf{x}\rangle$, and we denote its risk by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}):=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v})=\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}\rangle-y\big)^{2},\quad\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}.

By increasing $M$ and $N$, we have a sequence of datasets and trainable parameters of increasing sizes, respectively. This prepares us to study the scaling law Equation 1 in the sketched linear regression problem, that is, to understand $\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}_{N})$ as a function of both $M$ and $N$.

Risk decomposition.

In a standard way, we decompose the risk achieved by $\mathbf{v}_{N}$, the last iterate of (SGD), to the sum of irreducible risk, approximization error, and excess risk as follows,

\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}_{N})=\underbrace{\min\mathcal{R}(\cdot)}_{\mathsf{Irreducible}}+\underbrace{\min\mathcal{R}_{M}(\cdot)-\min\mathcal{R}(\cdot)}_{\mathsf{Approx}}+\underbrace{\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}_{N})-\min\mathcal{R}_{M}(\cdot)}_{\mathsf{Excess}}.

We emphasize that the irreducible risk is independent of $M$ and $N$ and thus can be viewed as a constant; the approximation error is determined by the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, thus depends on $M$ but is independent of $N$; the excess risk depends on both $M$ and $N$ as it is determined by the algorithm.

4 Scaling laws [body]
We first demonstrate a scaling-law behavior when the data spectrum satisfies a power law.

Assumption 1 (Distributional conditions).

Assume the following about the data distribution.

A. Gaussian design. Assume that $\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{H})$.

B. Well-specified model. Assume that $\mathbb{E}[y|\mathbf{x}]=\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{w}^{*}$. Define $\sigma^{2}:=\mathbb{E}(y-\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{w}^{*})^{2}$.

C. Parameter prior. Assume that $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ satisfies a prior such that $\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{w}^{*})^{\otimes 2}=\mathbf{I}$.

Assumption 2 (Power-law spectrum).

There exists $a>1$ such that the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ satisfy $\lambda_{i}\eqsim i^{-a}$, $i>0$.

Theorem 4.1 (Scaling law).

Suppose that 1 and 2 hold. Consider an $M$-dimensional sketched predictor trained by Equation SGD with $N$ samples. Let $N_{\mathtt{eff}}:=N/\log(N)$ and recall the risk decomposition in Equation 4. Then there exists some $a$-dependent constant $c>0$ such that when the initial stepsize $\gamma\leq c$, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, we have

1. $\mathsf{Irreducible}:=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}^{*})=\sigma^{2}$.

2. ${\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx}\eqsim M^{1-a}.$

3. Suppose in addition $\sigma^{2}\gtrsim 1$. The expected excess risk ($\mathsf{Excess}$) can be decomposed into a bias error ($\mathsf{Bias}$) and a variance error ($\mathsf{Var}$), namely, $\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathsf{Excess}\eqsim\mathsf{Bias}+\sigma^{2}\mathsf{Var},$ where the expectation is over the randomness of $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ and $(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{N}$. Moreover, $\mathsf{Bias}$ and $\mathsf{Var}$ satisfy $\displaystyle\mathsf{Bias}$ $\displaystyle\lesssim\max\big\{M^{1-a},\ (N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a-1}\big\},$ $\displaystyle\mathsf{Bias}$ $\displaystyle\gtrsim(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a-1}\text{ when }(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}\leq M/c\text{\hskip 10.22217ptfor some constant }c>0,$ $\displaystyle\mathsf{Var}$ $\displaystyle\eqsim{\min\big\{M,\ (N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}\big\}}/{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}.$

In all results, the hidden constants only depend on the power-law degree $a$. As a direct consequence, when $\sigma^{2}\eqsim 1$, it holds with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$ that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}_{N})=\sigma^{2}+\Theta\bigg(\frac{1}{M^{a-1}}\bigg)+\Theta\bigg(\frac{1}{(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{(a-1)/a}}\bigg),

where the expectation is over the randomness of $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ and $(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{N}$.

Theorem 4.1 shows a sharp (up to constant factors) scaling law risk bound under an isotroptic prior assumption and the power-law spectrum assumption. We emphasize that the scaling law bound in Theorem 4.1 holds for every $M,N\geq 1$. We also remark that the sum of approximization and bias errors dominates $\mathbb{E}\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}_{N})-\sigma^{2}$, whereas the variance error is of strict higher order in terms of both $M$ and $N$, and is thus disappeared in the population risk bound.

Optimal stepsize.

Based on the tight scaling law in Theorem 4.1, we can calculate the optimal stepsize that minimizes the risk. Specifically, the optimal stepsize is $\gamma\eqsim 1$ when $N_{\mathtt{eff}}\lesssim M^{a}$ and can be anything such that ${M^{a}}/{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}\lesssim\gamma\lesssim 1$ when $N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gtrsim M^{a}$. In both cases, choosing $\gamma\eqsim 1$ is optimal. When the sample size is large such that $N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gtrsim M^{a}$, the optimal stepsize is relatively robust and can be chosen from a range.

Allocation of data and model sizes.

Following Hoffmann et al. (2022), we measure the compute complexity by $MN$ as Equation SGD queries $M$-dimensional gradients for $N$ times. Given a total compute budget of $MN=C$, from Theorem 6.1 and $N_{\mathtt{eff}}:=N/\log(N)$, we see that the best population risk is achieved by setting $\gamma=\Theta(1)$, $M=\tilde{\Theta}(C^{1/(a+1)})$, and $N=\tilde{\Theta}(C^{a/(a+1)})$. Our theory suggests setting a data size slightly larger than the model size when the compute budget is the bottleneck.

Comparison with (Bordelon et al., 2024).

The work by Bordelon et al. (2024) considered the scaling law of batch gradient descent (or gradient flow) on a teacher-student model (see their equation (14)). Their teacher-student model can be viewed as our sketched linear regression model. However, we consider one-pass SGD, therefore in our setting the number of gradient steps is equivalent to the data size. When we equalize the number of gradient steps and the data size in their equation (14) and set the parameter prior as 1C, their prediction is consistent with ours. However, our analysis shows the computational advantage of SGD over batch GD since each iteration requires only $1/N$ the compute. Bordelon et al. (2024) obtained the limit of the population risk as two out of the data size, model size, and the number of gradient steps go to infinity based on statistical physics heuristics. In comparison, we obtain upper and lower risk bounds that hold for any finite $M$ and $N$ and match ignoring a constant factor depending only on the spectrum power-law degree $a$.

Average of the SGD iterates

Results similar to Theorem 4.1 can also be established for the average of the iterates of online SGD with constant stepsize (Polyak and Juditsky, 1992; Dieuleveut et al., 2017; Jain et al., 2018; Jain et al., 2017; Zou et al., 2023). All results will be the same once replacing the effective sample size $N_{\mathtt{eff}}$ in Theorem 4.1 to the sample size $N$. For more details see Theorem F.6 in Appendix F.

4 Scaling laws > 4.1 Scaling law under source condition [body]
The isotropic parameter prior condition (1C) in Theorem 4.1 can be generalized to the following anisotropic version (Caponnetto and De Vito, 2007).

Assumption 3 (Source condition).

Let $(\lambda_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})_{i>0}$ be the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of $\mathbf{H}$. Assume $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ satisfies a prior such that

\text{for $i\neq j$},\ \ {\mathbb{E}}\langle\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{w}^{*}\rangle\langle\mathbf{v}_{j},\mathbf{w}^{*}\rangle=0;\ \ \text{and for $i>0$},\ \ {\mathbb{E}}\lambda_{i}\langle\mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{w}^{*}\rangle^{2}\eqsim i^{-b},\ \text{ for some $b>1$}.

A larger exponent $b$ implies a faster decay of signal $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ and thus corresponds to a simpler task (Caponnetto and De Vito, 2007). Note that 1C satisfies Assumption 3 with $b=a$.

Theorem 4.2 (Scaling law under source condition).

In Theorem 4.1, suppose Assumption 1C is replaced by Assumption 3 with $1<b<a+1$. Then there exists some $a$-dependent constant $c>0$ such that when $\gamma\leq c$, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}_{N})=\sigma^{2}+\underbrace{\Theta\bigg(\frac{1}{M^{b-1}}\bigg)+\Theta\bigg(\frac{1}{(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{(b-1)/a}}\bigg)}_{\mathsf{Approx}+\mathsf{Bias}}+\underbrace{\Theta\bigg(\frac{\min\big\{M,\ (N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}\big\}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}\bigg)}_{\mathsf{Var}}.

where the expectation is over the randomness of $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ and $(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{N}$, and $\Theta(\cdot)$ hides constants that may depend on $(a,b)$.

When $1<b\leq a$, the tasks are relatively hard (compared to when $b=a$), and the variance error is dominated by the sum of approximation and bias errors for all choices of $M$, $N$, and $\gamma\lesssim 1$. In this case, Theorem 4.2 gives the same prediction about optimal stepsize and optimal allocation of data and model sizes under compute budget as Theorem 4.1.

When $a<b<a+1$, the tasks are relatively easy (compared to when $b=a$), and variance remains dominated by the sum of approximation and bias error if the stepsize is optimally tuned. Recall that $\gamma\lesssim 1$, thus we can rewrite the risk bound in Theorem 4.2 as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}_{N})-\sigma^{2} \displaystyle\eqsim\frac{1}{\min\big\{M,\ (N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}\big\}^{b-1}}+\frac{\min\big\{M,\ (N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}\big\}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}} \displaystyle\eqsim\begin{cases}{\min\big\{M,\ (N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}\big\}}/{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}&M\gtrsim N_{\mathtt{eff}}^{1/b}\ \text{and}\ N_{\mathtt{eff}}^{a/b-1}\lesssim\gamma\lesssim 1,\\ {\min\big\{M,\ (N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}\big\}^{1-b}}&M\lesssim N_{\mathtt{eff}}^{1/b}\ \text{or}\ \gamma\lesssim N_{\mathtt{eff}}^{a/b-1}.\end{cases}

Therefore the optimal stepsize and the risk under the optimal stepsize is

\displaystyle\gamma\eqsim N_{\mathtt{eff}}^{a/b-1}\text{ if }M\gtrsim N_{\mathtt{eff}}^{1/b},~~~~~\text{ and }\ M^{a}/N_{\mathtt{eff}}\lesssim\gamma\lesssim 1\text{ if }M\lesssim N_{\mathtt{eff}}^{1/b}.

Under the optimally tunned stepsize, the population risk is in the form of

\displaystyle\textstyle\min_{\gamma}\mathbb{E}\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}_{N})=\sigma^{2}+\Theta(N_{\mathtt{eff}}^{(1-b)/b})+\Theta(M^{1-b}),

which is again in the scaling law form Equation 1. This is expected since an optimally tuned stepsize controls the variance error by adjusting the strength of the implicit bias of SGD. Under a fixed compute budget $C=MN$, our theory suggests to assign $M=\tilde{\Theta}(C^{1/(b+1)})$ and $N=\tilde{\Theta}(C^{b/(b+1)})$, and set the stepsize to $\gamma\eqsim\tilde{\Theta}(C^{(a-b)/(b+1)})$.

When $b\geq a+1$, the tasks are even simpler. We provide upper and lower bounds in Appendix D.3. However, there exists a gap between the bounds, fixing which is left for future work.

Moreover, we note that in the comparable regimes where $M$ is large, the results in Theorem 4.2 match existing bounds on the risk of SGD iterates and ridge estimators (Pillaud-Vivien et al., 2018; Rudi and Rosasco, 2017).

4 Scaling laws > 4.2 Scaling law under logarithmic power law [body]
We also derive the risk formula when the data covariance has a logarithmic power-law spectrum (Bartlett et al., 2020).

Assumption 4 (Logarithmic power-law spectrum).

There exists $a>1$ such that the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ satisfy $\lambda_{i}\eqsim i^{-1}\log^{-a}(i+1)$, $i>0$.

Theorem 4.3 (Scaling law under logarithmic power spectrum).

In Theorem 4.1, suppose Assumption 2 is replaced by Assumption 4. Then with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}_{N})=\sigma^{2}+\Theta\bigg(\frac{1}{\log^{a-1}(M)}\bigg)+\Theta\bigg(\frac{1}{\log^{a-1}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\bigg),\quad\mathsf{Var}\eqsim\frac{\min\big\{M,\ \frac{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}{\log^{a}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\big\}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}},

where the expectation is over the randomness of $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ and $(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{N}$.

Theorem 4.3 provides a scaling law under the logarithmic power-law spectrum. Similar to Theorem 4.1, the variance error is dominated by the approximation and bias errors for all choices of $M$, $N$, and $\gamma$, and thus disappeared from the risk bound. Different from Theorem 4.1, here the population risk is a polynomial of $\log(M)$ and $\log(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)$.

6 Risk bounds under a general spectrum [body]
In this section, we present some general results on the upper and lower bounds of the risk of the output of (SGD). Due to the rotational invariance of the sketched matrix $\mathbf{S}$, without loss of generality, we assume the covariance $\mathbf{H}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d\times d}$ is diagonal with non-increasing diagonal entries. Our main results in Section 4 are directly built on the general bounds introduced here.

Assumption 5 (General distributional conditions).

Assume the following about the data distribution.

A. Hypercontractivity. There exists $\alpha\geq 1$ such that for every PSD matrix $\mathbf{A}$ it holds that $\mathbb{E}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{\top}\preceq\alpha\tr(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{A})\mathbf{H}.$

B. Misspecified model. There exists $\sigma^{2}>0$ such that $\mathbb{E}(y-\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{w}^{*})^{2}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{\top}\preceq\sigma^{2}\mathbf{H}$.

It is clear that Assumption 1 implies Assumption 5 with $\alpha=3$.

Excess risk decomposition.

Conditioning on the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, the training of the sketched linear predictor can be viewed as an $M$-dimensional linear regression problem. We can therefore invoke existing SGD analysis (Wu et al., 2022a; Wu et al., 2022b) to sharply control the excess risk by controlling the bias and variance errors. Specifically, let us define the ($\mathbf{w}^{*}$-dependent) bias error as

\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}):=\bigg\|\prod_{t=1}^{N}\big(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big){\mathbf{v}^{*}}\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}},\quad\text{where}\ \mathbf{v}^{*}:=(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{w}^{*},

and the variance error as

\mathsf{Var}:=\frac{\#\{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\geq 1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)\}+(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{2}\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}^{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}},\quad N_{\mathtt{eff}}:=N/\log(N),

where $\big(\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\big)_{j=1}^{M}$ are eigenvalues of $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$. We also let $\mathsf{Bias}:={\mathbb{E}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})$, where the expectation is over the prior of $\mathbf{w}^{*}.$ Using the existing results on the output of (SGD) in Wu et al. (2022a); Wu et al. (2022b), we show that the excess risk in (4) can be exactly decomposed as the sum of bias and variance errors under weak conditions.

Theorem 6.1 (Excess risk decomposition).

Conditioning on the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, consider the excess risk in Equation 4 induced by the output of Equation SGD. Assume $\mathbf{v}_{0}=0$. Then for any $\mathbf{w}^{*}\in\mathbb{H}$,

1. Under 5A and 5B and suppose $\gamma\leq{1}/{\big(c\alpha\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})\big)}$ for some constant $c>0$, we have $\mathbb{E}\mathsf{Excess}\lesssim\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\big(\alpha\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}+\sigma^{2}\big)\mathsf{Var}.\vskip-3.00003pt$

2. Under the stronger 1A and 1B and suppose $\gamma\leq{1}/{\big(c\alpha\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}))}$ for some constant $c>0$, we have $\mathbb{E}\mathsf{Excess}\gtrsim\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})+\sigma^{2}\mathsf{Var}.$

In both results, the expectations of $\mathsf{Excess}$ are taken over $(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t})_{t=1}^{N}$.

Assuming that the signal-to-noise ratio is upper bounded, that is, $\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}/\sigma^{2}\lesssim 1$, then the bias-variance decomposition of the excess risk is sharp up to constant factors.

The variance error is in a nice form and can be computed using the following important lemma on the spectrum of $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$. Similar results for logarithmic power-law are also established in Lemma G.6 in Appendix G.

Lemma 6.2 (Power law).

Under Assumption 2, it holds with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ that

\displaystyle\mu_{j}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})\eqsim\mu_{j}(\mathbf{H})\eqsim j^{-a},\quad j=1,\dots,M.

For any $0\leq k^{*}\leq k^{\dagger}\leq\infty$, let $\mathbf{S}_{k^{*}:k^{\dagger}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times(k^{\dagger}-k^{*})}$ denote the matrix formed by the $k^{*}+1-k^{\dagger}$-th columns of $\mathbf{S}$. We also abuse the notation $k^{\dagger}:\infty$ for $k^{\dagger}:d$ when $d$ is finite. We let $\mathbf{H}_{k^{*}:k^{\dagger}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{(k^{\dagger}-k^{*})\times(k^{\dagger}-k^{*})}$ be the submatrix of $\mathbf{H}$ formed by the $k^{*}+1-k^{\dagger}$-th eigenvalues. For the approximation and bias error, we use the following upper and lower bounds to compute their values.

Theorem 6.3 (A general upper bound).

Suppose Assumption 5 holds. Assume $\mathbf{v}_{0}=0$, $r(\mathbf{H})\geq 2M$ and the initial stepsize satisfies $\gamma<1/(c\alpha\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}))$ for some constant $c>0.$ Then for any $k_{1},k_{2}\leq M/3$, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle\lesssim\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k_{1}:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k_{1}:\infty}}}+\bigg(\frac{\sum_{i>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}}{M}+\lambda_{k_{1}+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\bigg)\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k_{1}}}}\|^{2}, \displaystyle\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k_{2}}}}\|_{2}^{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}\cdot\Bigg[\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{S}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k_{2}:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k_{2}:\infty})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k_{2}:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k_{2}:\infty})}\Bigg]^{2}+\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}.

Theorem 6.4 (A general lower bound).

Suppose Assumption 1 holds. Assume $\mathbf{v}_{0}=0$, $r(\mathbf{H})\geq M$ and the initial stepsize $\gamma<1/\big(c\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})\big)$ for some constant $c>0.$ Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx}\gtrsim\sum_{i=M}^{d}\lambda_{i},~~~~~{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\gtrsim\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{2}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}

almost surely, where $(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{d}$ are eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ in non-increasing order, $(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})_{i=1}^{M}$ are eigenvalues of $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$ in non-increasing order.

7 Conclusion [body]
We analyze neural scaling laws in infinite-dimensional linear regression. We consider a linear predictor with $M$ trainable parameters on the sketched covariates, which is trained by one-pass stochastic gradient descent with $N$ data. Under a Gaussian prior assumption on the optimal model parameter and a power law (of degree $a>1$) assumption on the spectrum of the data covariance, we derive matching upper and lower bounds on the population risk minus the irreducible error, that is, $\Theta(M^{-(a-1)}+N^{-(a-1)/a})$. In particular, we show that the variance error, which increases with $M$, is of strictly higher order compared to the other errors, thus disappearing from the risk bound. We attribute the nice empirical formula of the neural scaling law to the non-domination of the variance error, which ultimately is an effect of the implicit regularization of SGD.

Many directions remain open for future study. First, our work is limited to the linear model; it would be interesting to see whether similar scaling laws can be derived in more complex models, such as random feature models or two-layer networks. Second, we focus on one-pass SGD training, and it is unclear if similar results hold for other optimization methods like accelerated SGD or Adam. Additionally, from a technical perspective, many results in our work depend on the Gaussian assumption and the source condition of the data. Investigating how these assumptions can be relaxed would also be valuable.

\mathbf{v}^{*}:=(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{w}^{*},

and the approximation error in Equation 4 is

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle:=\min\mathcal{R}_{M}(\cdot)-\min\mathcal{R}(\cdot) \displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Big\|^{2}.

Moreover, $\mathsf{Approx}\leq\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}$ almost surely over the randomness of $\mathbf{S}$.

Proof of Lemma A.1.

Recall that the risk

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}):=\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}\rangle-y\big)^{2}

is a quadratic function and that $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ is the minimizer of $\mathcal{R}(\cdot)$, so we have

\displaystyle\big(\mathbb{E}\mathbf{x}^{\otimes 2}\big)\mathbf{w}^{*}=\mathbb{E}\mathbf{x}y\Leftrightarrow\mathbf{H}\mathbf{w}^{*}=\mathbb{E}\mathbf{x}y,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}) \displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}^{*}\rangle\big)^{2}+\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle=\|\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{*})\|^{2}+\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}^{*}).

Recall that the risk in a restricted subspace

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}):=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v})=\mathbb{E}\big(\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}\rangle-y\big)^{2}

is also a quadratic function, so its minimizer is given by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{v}^{*}} \displaystyle=\big(\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x})^{\otimes 2}\big)^{-1}\mathbb{E}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}y \displaystyle=\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}.

Therefore, the approximation error is

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle:=\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v}^{*})-\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*})-\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle=\|\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*}-\mathbf{w}^{*})\|^{2} \displaystyle=\|\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}-\mathbf{w}^{*})\|^{2} \displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Big\|^{2}.

Finally, since

\displaystyle\Big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)^{2}=\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\preceq\mathbf{I},

it follows that $\mathsf{Approx}\leq\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}$. ∎

Appendix A Preliminary > A.3 Bias-variance decomposition [body]
The excess risk in Equation 4 can be viewed as the SGD excess risk in an $M$-dimensional (misspecified) linear regression problem. We will utilize Corollary 3.4 in (Wu et al., 2022b) to get a bias-variance decomposition of the excess risk. The following two lemmas check the related assumptions for Corollary 3.4 in (Wu et al., 2022b) in our setup.

Lemma A.2 (Hypercontractivity and the misspecified noise under sketched feature).

Suppose that 5A and 5B hold. Conditioning on the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, for every PSD matrix $\mathbf{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times M}$, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x})^{\otimes 2}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x})^{\otimes 2}\preceq\alpha\tr\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\big)\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}.

Moreover, for the minimizer of $\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v})$, that is, $\mathbf{v}^{*}$ defined in Lemma A.1, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(y-\langle\mathbf{v}^{*},\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}\rangle\big)^{2}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x})^{\otimes 2}\preceq 2(\sigma^{2}+\alpha\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}})\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}.

The expectation in the above is over $(\mathbf{x},y)$.

Proof of Lemma A.2.

The first part is a direct application of 5A:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x})^{\otimes 2}\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x})^{\otimes 2} \displaystyle=\mathbf{S}\big(\mathbb{E}\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{S})\mathbf{x}\mathbf{x}^{\top}\big)\mathbf{S}^{\top} \displaystyle\preceq\mathbf{S}\big(\alpha\tr\big(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{S}\big)\mathbf{H}\big)\mathbf{S}^{\top} \displaystyle=\alpha\tr\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\big)\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}.

For the second part, we first show that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}\big(y-\langle\mathbf{v}^{*},\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}\rangle\big)^{2}\mathbf{x}^{\otimes 2}} \displaystyle\preceq 2\mathbb{E}\big(y-\langle\mathbf{w}^{*},\mathbf{x}\rangle\big)^{2}\mathbf{x}^{\otimes 2}+2\mathbb{E}\langle\mathbf{w}^{*}-\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*},\mathbf{x}\rangle^{2}\mathbf{x}^{\otimes 2} \displaystyle\preceq 2\sigma^{2}\mathbf{H}+2\alpha\langle\mathbf{H},(\mathbf{w}^{*}-\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*})^{\otimes 2}\rangle\mathbf{H},

where the last inequality is by 5A and 5B. From the proof of Lemma A.1, we know that

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{H},(\mathbf{w}^{*}-\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*})^{\otimes 2}\rangle=\mathsf{Approx}\leq\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}},\ \text{almost surely}.

So we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big(y-\langle\mathbf{v}^{*},\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}\rangle\big)^{2}\mathbf{x}^{\otimes 2}\preceq 2(\sigma^{2}+\alpha\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}})\mathbf{H}.

Left and right multiplying both sides with $\mathbf{S}$ and $\mathbf{S}^{\top}$, we obtain the second claim. ∎

Lemma A.3 (Gaussianity and well-specified noise under sketched features).

Suppose that 1A and 1B hold. Conditional on the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, we have

\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}).

Moreover, for the minimizer of $\mathcal{R}_{M}(\mathbf{v})$, that is, $\mathbf{v}^{*}$ defined in Lemma A.1, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[y|\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}]=\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle,\quad\mathbb{E}(y-\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle)^{2}=\sigma^{2}+\mathsf{Approx}\geq\sigma^{2}.

Proof of Lemma A.3.

The first claim is a direct consequence of 1A.

For the second claim, by 1A and Lemma A.1, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[y|\mathbf{x}] \displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}^{*}\rangle \displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{w}^{*}-\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle \displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{x},\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{x},\big[\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\big]\mathbf{w}^{*}\rangle \displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle+\langle\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{x},\big[\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}^{*}\rangle \displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle+\langle\big[\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}^{*}\rangle.

Notice that

\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}),

by 1A and that

\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big[\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]=0,

therefore

\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{x}\ \text{is independent of}\ \big[\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big]\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{x}.

Taking expectation over the second random vector in Equation 8, we find

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[y|\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}] \displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\mathbb{E}[y|\mathbf{x}]=\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle.

It remains to show

\mathbb{E}(y-\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle)^{2}=\sigma^{2}+\mathsf{Approx}.

This follows from the proof of Lemma A.1. Specifically,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(y-\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x},\mathbf{v}^{*}\rangle)^{2} \displaystyle=\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*}) \displaystyle=\mathsf{Approx}+\mathcal{R}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle=\mathsf{Approx}+\sigma^{2} \displaystyle\geq\sigma^{2},

where the second equality is by the definition of $\mathsf{Approx}$ and the third equality is by 1B. We have completed the proof. ∎

We now use the results in (Wu et al., 2022a; Wu et al., 2022b) for SGD to obtain the following bias-variance decomposition on the excess risk.

Theorem A.4 (Excess risk bounds).

Consider the excess risk in Equation 4 induced by the output of Equation SGD. Let

N_{\mathtt{eff}}:=N/\log(N),\quad\mathtt{SNR}:=(\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}+\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}}^{2})/\sigma^{2}.

Then conditioning on the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, for any $\mathbf{w}^{*}\in\mathbb{H}$

1. Under 5A and 5B, we have $\mathbb{E}\mathsf{Excess}\lesssim\bigg\|\prod_{t=1}^{N}\big(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)(\mathbf{v}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}^{*}})\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}}+(1+\alpha\mathtt{SNR})\sigma^{2}\cdot\frac{D_{\mathtt{eff}}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}$ when $\gamma\lesssim\frac{1}{c\alpha\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}$ for some constant $c>0.$

2. Under 1A and 1B, we have $\mathbb{E}\mathsf{Excess}\gtrsim\bigg\|\prod_{t=1}^{N}\big(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)(\mathbf{v}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}^{*}})\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}}+\sigma^{2}\cdot\frac{D_{\mathtt{eff}}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}$ when $\gamma\lesssim\frac{1}{c\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}$ for some constant $c>0.$

In both results, the expectation is over $(\mathbf{x}_{t},y_{t})_{t=1}^{N}$, and

\displaystyle D_{\mathtt{eff}}:=\#\{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\geq 1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)\}+(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{2}\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}^{2},

where $(\tilde{\lambda}_{j})_{j=1}^{M}$ are eigenvalue of $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$.

Theorem 6.1 follows immediately by Lemma A.1 and by setting $\mathbf{v}_{0}=0$ and plugging the definition of $\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})$ and $\mathsf{Var}$ into Theorem A.4.

Proof of Theorem A.4.

This follows from Corollary 3.4 in (Wu et al., 2022b) for a linear regression problem with population data given by $(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x},y)$. Note that the data covariance becomes $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$ and the optimal model parameter becomes $\mathbf{v}^{*}$.

For the upper bound, Lemma A.2 verifies Assumptions 1A and 2 in (Wu et al., 2022b), with the noise level being

\tilde{\sigma}^{2}=2(\sigma^{2}+\alpha\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}).

Then we can apply the upper bound in Corollary 3.4 in (Wu et al., 2022b) (setting their index set $\mathbb{K}=\emptyset$) to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathsf{Excess}\lesssim\bigg\|\prod_{t=1}^{N}\big(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)(\mathbf{v}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}^{*}})\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}}+(\|\mathbf{v}^{*}-\mathbf{v}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}}+\tilde{\sigma}^{2})\frac{D_{\mathtt{eff}}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}.

We verify that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}^{*}-\mathbf{v}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}} \displaystyle\leq 2\|\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{v}^{*}\|^{2}+2\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}} \displaystyle=2\|\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}+2\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}} \displaystyle\leq 2\|\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}+2\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}} \displaystyle=2\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}+2\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}},

which implies that

\displaystyle(\|\mathbf{v}^{*}-\mathbf{v}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}}+\tilde{\sigma}^{2}) \displaystyle\leq 2\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}+2\|\mathbf{v}_{0}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}}+2(\sigma^{2}+\alpha\|\mathbf{w}^{*}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}}) \displaystyle\lesssim(1+\alpha\mathtt{SNR})\sigma^{2}.

Substituting, we get the upper bound.

For the lower bound, Lemma A.3 shows $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{x}$ is Gaussian, therefore it satisfies Assumption 1B in Wu et al. (2022b) with $\beta=1$. Besides, Lemma A.3 shows that the linear regression problem is well-specified, with the noise level being

\tilde{\sigma}^{2}=\sigma^{2}+\mathsf{Approx}\geq\sigma^{2}.

Although the lower bound in Corollary 3.4 in Wu et al. (2022b) is stated for Gaussian additive noise (see their Assumption 2’), it is easy to check that the lower bound holds for any well-specified noise as described by Lemma A.3. Using the lower bound in Corollary 3.4 in Wu et al. (2022b), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathsf{Excess}\gtrsim\bigg\|\prod_{t=1}^{N}\big(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)(\mathbf{v}_{0}-{\mathbf{v}^{*}})\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}}+\tilde{\sigma}^{2}\frac{D_{\mathtt{eff}}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}.

Plugging in $\tilde{\sigma}^{2}\geq\sigma^{2}$ gives the desired lower bound. ∎

Appendix C Approximation error > C.1 An upper bound [body]
Lemma C.1 (An upper bound on the approximation error).

Given any $k\leq d$ such that $r(\mathbf{H})\geq k+M$, the approximation error in (4) (and 7) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx}\lesssim\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}}+\langle[\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1},{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}^{\top}\rangle

almost surely, where $\mathbf{A}_{k}:=\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}^{\top}$. If in addition $k\leq M/2$, then with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx}\lesssim\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}}+\Big(\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}+\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Big)\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}\|^{2},

where $(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{p}$ are eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ in non-increasing order.

Proof of Lemma C.1.

Write the singular value decomposition $\mathbf{H}=\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}\mathbf{U}^{\top}$, where $\bm{\Lambda}:=\diag\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d}\}$ with $\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\ldots\lambda_{d}\geq 0$ and $\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}=\mathbf{I}$. Define $\tilde{\mathbf{S}}:=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{U},\tilde{\mathbf{w}}^{*}:=\mathbf{U}^{\top}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}$. Then by Lemma A.1 the approximation error $\mathsf{Approx}=\mathsf{Approx}(\mathbf{S},\mathbf{H},{\mathbf{w}^{*}})$ satisfies

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx}(\mathbf{S},\mathbf{H},{\mathbf{w}^{*}}) \displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Big\|^{2} \displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\big(\tilde{\mathbf{S}}\bm{\Lambda}\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\Big)\mathbf{U}\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{U}^{\top}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Big\|^{2} \displaystyle=\Big\|\mathbf{U}\Big(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\big(\tilde{\mathbf{S}}\bm{\Lambda}\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{U}^{\top}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Big\|^{2} \displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\mathbf{I}-\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\big(\tilde{\mathbf{S}}\bm{\Lambda}\tilde{\mathbf{S}}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\bm{\Lambda}^{\frac{1}{2}}\tilde{\mathbf{w}}^{*}\Big\|^{2}=\mathsf{Approx}(\tilde{\mathbf{S}},\bm{\Lambda},\tilde{\mathbf{w}}^{*}).

Since $\tilde{\mathbf{S}}\overset{d}{=}\mathbf{S}$ by rotational invariance of standard Gaussian variables, it suffices to analyze the case where $\mathbf{H}=\bm{\Lambda}$ is a diagonal matrix, as the results may transfer to general $\mathbf{H}$ by replacing $\tilde{\mathbf{w}}^{*}$ with ${\mathbf{w}^{*}}$.

Therefore, from now on we assume w.l.o.g. that $\mathbf{H}$ is a diagonal matrix with non-increasing diagonal entries. Define $\mathbf{A}:=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$.

By definition of $\mathsf{Approx}$, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Big\|^{2} \displaystyle=\big\langle[\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}-\mathbf{I}_{d}]^{\otimes 2},\mathbf{H}^{1/2}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\big\rangle.

Moreover, for any $k\in[d]$

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}-\mathbf{I}_{d} \displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\\ \mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}&\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\end{pmatrix}-\mathbf{I}_{d} \displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}-\mathbf{I}_{k}&\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\\ \mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}&\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}-\mathbf{I}_{d-k}\end{pmatrix} \displaystyle=:\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{U}}&{\mathsf{V}}\\ {\mathsf{V}}^{\top}&{\mathsf{W}}\end{pmatrix}

Therefore

\displaystyle[\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}-\mathbf{I}_{p}]^{\otimes 2}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}&{\mathsf{U}}{\mathsf{V}}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{W}}\\ {\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{U}}+{\mathsf{W}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}&{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}}\end{pmatrix}\preceq 2\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}&{\mathbf{0}}\\ {\mathbf{0}}&{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}}\end{pmatrix},

and hence

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle\leq 2\Bigg\langle\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}&{\mathbf{0}}\\ {\mathbf{0}}&{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}}\end{pmatrix},\mathbf{H}^{1/2}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\Bigg\rangle \displaystyle=2\big\langle{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top},\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{1/2}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{0:k}}}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{0:k}}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{1/2}\big\rangle+2\big\langle{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}},\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}^{1/2}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{k:\infty}}}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{k:\infty}}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}^{1/2}\big\rangle.

We claim the following results which we will prove at the end of the proof.

\displaystyle\big\langle{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}},\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}^{1/2}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{k:\infty}}}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{k:\infty}}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}^{1/2}\big\rangle \displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}}, \displaystyle\big\langle{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top},\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{1/2}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{0:k}}}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{0:k}}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{1/2}\big\rangle \displaystyle=\langle[\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1},{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}^{\top}\rangle.

Note that in claim (11) the inverse $\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}$ exists almost surely since $r(\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}})\geq r(\mathbf{H})-k\geq M$ by our assumption and $\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times(d-k)}$ is a random gaussian projection onto ${\mathbb{R}}^{M}$. First part of Lemma C.1 follows immediately from combining claim (10) and (11).

To prove the second part of Lemma C.1, first note that with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ we have

\displaystyle{\mu_{\min}}(\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1})=\|\mathbf{A}_{k}\|^{-1}\geq c/\Big(\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}+\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Big)

forc some constant $c>0$ by Lemma G.2. Moreover, by the concentration of the Gaussian variance matrix (see e.g., Theorem 6.1 in Wainwright (2019)), we have $\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\succeq\mathbf{I}_{k}/5$ with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ when $M/k\geq 2$. Combining the last two arguments, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}} \displaystyle\succeq c\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}/\Big(\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}+\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Big) \displaystyle\gtrsim\mathbf{I}_{k}/\Big(\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}+\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Big),

and therefore

\displaystyle\langle[\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1},{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}^{\top}\rangle \displaystyle\leq\langle[\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1},{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}^{\top}\rangle \displaystyle\leq\|[\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\|\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}\|^{2} \displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}+\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Big)\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}\|^{2}

with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Combining Eq. (12) with the first part of Lemma C.1 completes the proof.

Proof of claim (10)

Note that

\displaystyle-\mathbf{I}_{d-k}\preceq{\mathsf{W}} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}-\mathbf{I}_{d-k} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}(\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}-\mathbf{I}_{d-k} \displaystyle\preceq\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}-\mathbf{I}_{d-k}\preceq{\mathbf{0}}_{d-k},

where the last inequality uses the fact that the norm of projection matrices is no greater than one. Therefore, we have $\|{\mathsf{W}}\|_{2}\leq 1$. Now, it remains to show

\displaystyle{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}}=-{\mathsf{W}},

as claim (10) is a direct consequence of Eq. (13) and the fact that $\|{\mathsf{W}}\|\leq 1$.

By definition of ${\mathsf{W}}$ in Eq. (9), we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{W}}^{2} \displaystyle=(\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}-\mathbf{I}_{d-k})^{2} \displaystyle=\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-2\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}+\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}} \displaystyle=\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-2\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}+\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{A}_{k}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}.

By definition of ${\mathsf{V}}$ in Eq. (9), we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}(\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}})\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}.

Since $\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{A}_{k}=\mathbf{A}$, it follows that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}} \displaystyle=\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-2\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}+\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}} \displaystyle=\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}=-{\mathsf{W}}.

Proof of claim (11)

It suffices to show ${\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}}=[\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}$. Using the definition of ${\mathsf{U}}$ in Eq. (9), we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathsf{U}} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}-\mathbf{I}_{k} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}-\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}-\mathbf{I}_{k} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}-\mathbf{I}_{k},

where the second line uses Woodbury’s matrix identity, namely

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}^{-1}=[\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{0:k}+\mathbf{A}_{k}]^{-1}=\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}-\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}.

Continuing the calculation of ${\mathsf{U}}$, we have

\displaystyle{\mathsf{U}} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}-\mathbf{I}_{k} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}(\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}-\mathbf{I}_{k})\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}} \displaystyle=-\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathsf{U}}^{2}=\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}.

Since

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}^{-1} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}-\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}

by Woodbury’s matrix indentity, it follows from the definition of ${\mathsf{V}}$ in Eq. (9) that

\displaystyle{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}})\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}} \displaystyle=\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}.

Combining Eq. (14) and (15) yields

\displaystyle{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}=\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}},

and therefore

\displaystyle\big\langle{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top},\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{1/2}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{0:k}}}\mathbf{w}_{*,{{0:k}}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{1/2}\big\rangle \displaystyle=\langle[\mathbf{H}_{{0:k}}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1},{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}^{\top}\rangle.

∎

Appendix C Approximation error > C.2 A lower bound [body]
For the approximation error $\mathsf{Approx}$, we have the following result.

Lemma C.2 (Lower bound on the approximation error).

When $r(\mathbf{H})\geq M$, under 1C, the approximation error in (4) (and 7) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Approx}\gtrsim\sum_{i=M}^{d}\lambda_{i},

where $(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{d}$ are eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ in non-increasing order.

Proof of Lemma C.2.

For any $k\leq d$, following the proof of Lemma C.1, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle=\big\langle[\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}-\mathbf{I}_{d}]^{\otimes 2},\mathbf{H}^{1/2}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}(\mathbf{w}^{*})^{\top}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\big\rangle

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}-\mathbf{I}_{d} \displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}-\mathbf{I}_{k}&\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\\ \mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{0:k}}&\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}-\mathbf{I}_{d-k}\end{pmatrix} \displaystyle=:\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{U}}&{\mathsf{V}}\\ {\mathsf{V}}^{\top}&{\mathsf{W}}\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore

\displaystyle[\mathbf{H}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}-\mathbf{I}_{d}]^{\otimes 2}=\begin{pmatrix}{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}&{\mathsf{U}}{\mathsf{V}}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{W}}\\ {\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{U}}+{\mathsf{W}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}&{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}}\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\big\langle{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top},\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\big\rangle+\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\big\langle{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}},\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}\big\rangle \displaystyle\quad+2\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\big\langle{\mathsf{U}}{\mathsf{V}}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{W}},\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}\big\rangle \displaystyle=\tr(({\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top})\mathbf{H}_{0:k})+\tr(({\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}})\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}),

where the last line uses the fact that $\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}(\mathbf{w}^{*})^{\otimes 2}=\mathbf{I}_{d}.$ Using Eq. (13) and (16) in the proof of Lemma C.1, we further obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle=\tr(\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k})-\tr({\mathsf{W}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle=\tr([\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1})-\tr({\mathsf{W}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\geq-\tr({\mathsf{W}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty})=:T_{3}.

where $\mathbf{A}_{k}:=\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}.$ For $T_{3}$, we further have

\displaystyle T_{3} \displaystyle=\tr(\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}[\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}]\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}) \displaystyle\geq\tr(\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}[\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}]\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}) \displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d-k}\mu_{i}(\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}})\cdot\mu_{d+1-k-i}(\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}),

where the second line is due to $\mathbf{A}\succeq\mathbf{A}_{k}$ (and hence $-\mathbf{A}^{-1}\succeq-\mathbf{A}^{-1}_{k}$ ), the third line follows from Von-Neuman’s inequality. Since $\mathbf{M}:=\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}$ is a projection matrix such that $\mathbf{M}^{2}=\mathbf{M}$ and $\tr(\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-\mathbf{M})=M$, it follows that $\mathbf{M}$ has $M$ eigenvalues $0$ and $d-k-M$ eigenvalues $1$. Therefore, we further have

\displaystyle T_{3} \displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d-k}\mu_{i}(\mathbf{M})\cdot\mu_{d+1-k-i}(\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty})\geq\sum_{i=k+M}^{d}\lambda_{i}

for any $k\leq d$. Letting $k=0$ maximizes the lower bound and concludes the proof. ∎

Appendix C Approximation error > C.3 A lower bound under Assumption 3 [body]
Lemma C.3 (Lower bound on the approximation error under Assumption 3).

Under Assumption 3, the approximation error in (4) (and 7) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Approx}\gtrsim\sum_{i=M}^{d}\lambda_{i}i^{a-b},

where $(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{d}$ are eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ in non-increasing order and the inequality hides some $(a,b)$-dependent constant.

Proof of Lemma C.3.

The proof is essentially the same as the proof of Lemma C.2 but we include it here for completeness. Let $\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}:={\mathbb{E}}[\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{*\top}]$ be the covariance of the prior on $\mathbf{w}^{*}$. Following the proof of Lemma C.2, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\big\langle{\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top},\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\big\rangle+\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\big\langle{\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}},\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}\big\rangle \displaystyle\quad+2\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\big\langle{\mathsf{U}}{\mathsf{V}}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{W}},\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}\big\rangle \displaystyle=\tr(({\mathsf{U}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}{\mathsf{V}}^{\top})\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}_{0:k})+\tr(({\mathsf{W}}^{2}+{\mathsf{V}}^{\top}{\mathsf{V}})\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}_{k:\infty}),

where the last line uses Assumption 3 and notice that $\mathbf{H},\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}$ are both diagonal. Next, similar to the proof of Lemma C.2, using Eq. (13) and (16), we derive

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle=\tr(\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{-1/2}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}_{0:k})-\tr({\mathsf{W}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle=\tr([\mathbf{H}^{-1}_{{0:k}}+\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}_{0:k})-\tr({\mathsf{W}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\geq-\tr({\mathsf{W}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}_{k:\infty})=:{\tilde{T}}_{3}

where $\mathbf{A}_{k}:=\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}$. For ${\tilde{T}}_{3}$, following the same argument for $T_{3}$ in the proof of Lemma C.2, we have

\displaystyle{\tilde{T}}_{3} \displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{d-k}\mu_{i}(\mathbf{I}_{d-k}-\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{{k:\infty}})\cdot\mu_{d+1-k-i}(\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=k+M}^{d}\mu_{i}(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}})\gtrsim\sum_{i=k+M}^{d}i^{a-b}\lambda_{i},

for any $k\leq d$ where the last inequality uses Assumption 3. Setting $k=0$ maximizes the lower bound and concludes the proof. ∎

Appendix C Approximation error > C.4 Examples on matching bounds for $\mathsf{Approx}$ [body]
In this section, we derive matching upper and lower bounds for $\mathsf{Approx}$ (defined in Eq. 4 and 7) in three concrete examples: power-law spectrum (Lemma C.4), power-law spectrum with source condition (Lemma C.5) and logarithmic power-law spectrum (Lemma D.5).

Lemma C.4 (Bounds on $\mathsf{Approx}$ under the power-law spectrum).

Suppose Assumption 1C and 2 hold. Then with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of $\mathbf{S}$

\displaystyle M^{1-a}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx}\lesssim M^{1-a}.

Here, the hidden constants only depend on the power-law degree $a.$

Proof of Lemma C.4.

For the upper bound, by Lemma C.1 and noting ${\mathbb{E}}\mathbf{w}^{*2}_{i}=1$ for all $i$, we have with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}+\bigg(\frac{\sum_{i>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}}{M}+\lambda_{k_{1}+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\bigg)\cdot k_{1} \displaystyle\lesssim k_{1}^{1-a}+\bigg(\frac{k_{1}^{1-a}}{M}+k_{1}^{-a}+\sqrt{\frac{k_{1}^{1-2a}}{M}}\bigg)k_{1} \displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{k_{1}}{M}+1\bigg)k_{1}^{1-a}

for any given $k_{1}\leq M/2$. Here the hidden constants depend on $a$. Therefore, letting $k_{1}=M/2$ in the upper bound yields

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle\lesssim M^{1-{a}}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$.

For the lower bound, we have from Lemma C.2 that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Approx}\gtrsim\sum_{i=M}^{\infty}i^{-a}\gtrsim M^{1-{a}}.

This completes the proof. ∎

Lemma C.5 (Bounds on $\mathsf{Approx}$ under the source condition).

Suppose Assumption 3 hold. Then with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of $\mathbf{S}$

\displaystyle M^{1-b}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx}\lesssim M^{1-b}.

Here, the hidden constants only depend on the power-law degrees $a,b.$

Proof of Lemma C.5.

For the upper bound, by Lemma C.1 and noting ${\mathbb{E}}\mathbf{w}^{*2}_{i}\eqsim i^{a-b}$ for all $i$, we have with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}i^{a-b}+\bigg(\frac{\sum_{i>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}}{M}+\lambda_{k_{1}+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\bigg)\cdot k_{1}^{1+a-b} \displaystyle\lesssim k_{1}^{1-b}+\bigg(\frac{k_{1}^{1-a}}{M}+k_{1}^{-a}+\sqrt{\frac{k_{1}^{1-2a}}{M}}\bigg)k_{1}^{1+a-b} \displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\frac{k_{1}}{M}+1\bigg)k_{1}^{1-b}

for any given $k_{1}\leq M/2$. Here the hidden constants depend on $a,b$. Moreover, choosing $k_{1}=M/2$ in the upper bound gives

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle\lesssim M^{1-b}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$.

For the lower bound, we have from Lemma C.3 that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Approx}\gtrsim\sum_{i=M}^{\infty}i^{-a}\cdot i^{a-b}\gtrsim M^{1-b}.

This completes the proof. ∎

Lemma C.6 (Bounds on $\mathsf{Approx}$ under the logarithmic power-law spectrum).

Suppose Assumption 4 hold. Then with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of $\mathbf{S}$

\displaystyle\log^{1-a}M\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx}\lesssim\log^{1-a}M.

Here, the hidden constants only depend on the power-law degree $a.$

Proof of Lemma C.6.

For the upper bound, by Lemma C.1 and noting ${\mathbb{E}}\mathbf{w}^{*2}_{i}=1$ for all $i$, we have with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}+\bigg(\frac{\sum_{i>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}}{M}+\lambda_{k_{1}+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\bigg)k_{1} \displaystyle\lesssim\log^{1-a}k_{1}+\bigg(\frac{\log^{1-a}k_{1}}{M}+k_{1}^{-1}\log^{-a}k_{1}+\sqrt{\frac{k_{1}^{1-2a}}{M}}\bigg)k_{1} \displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(1+\frac{k_{1}}{M}+\frac{1}{\log k_{1}}+\frac{1}{\log k_{1}}\sqrt{\frac{k_{1}}{M}}\bigg)\log^{1-a}k_{1} \displaystyle\lesssim\log^{1-a}k_{1}

for any given $k_{1}\leq M/2$, where the third line uses $\sum_{i>k_{1}}\lambda_{i}^{2}\lesssim 1/(k_{1}\log^{2a}k_{1})$. Choosing $k_{1}=M/2$, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle\lesssim\log^{1-a}M

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Here the hidden constants depend on $a,b$.

For the lower bound, we have from Lemma C.2 that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Approx}\gtrsim\sum_{i=M}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\gtrsim\sum_{i=M}^{\infty}i^{-1}\log^{-a}i\gtrsim\log^{1-a}M.

Therefore, we have established matching upper and lower bounds for $\mathsf{Approx}.$ ∎

Appendix D Bias error [body]
In this section, we derive upper and lower bounds for $\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})$ defined in Eq. (5). Moreover, we show that the upper and lower bounds match up to constant factors in concrete examples.

Appendix D Bias error > D.1 An upper bound [body]
Lemma D.1 (Upper bound on the bias term).

Suppose the initial stepsize $\gamma\leq\frac{1}{c\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}$ for some constant $c>1$. Then for any $\mathbf{w}^{*}\in\mathbb{H}$ and $k\in[d]$ such that $r(\mathbf{H})\geq k+M$, the bias term in (5) satisfies

\displaystyle\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\frac{1}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}\|{\mathbf{v}^{*}}\|_{2}^{2}.

Moreover, for any $k\leq M/3$ such that $r(\mathbf{H})\geq k+M$, the bias term satisfies

\displaystyle\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\frac{\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}\|_{2}^{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}\cdot\Bigg[\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{A}_{k})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{A}_{k})}\Bigg]^{2}+\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}}

with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, where $\mathbf{A}_{k}:=\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}^{\top}$, $\{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{A}_{k})\}_{i=1}^{M}$ denote the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}_{k}$ in non-increasing order for some constant $c>1.$

Proof of Lemma D.1.

Similar to the proof of Lemma C.1, we can without loss of generality assume the covariance matrix $\mathbf{H}=\diag\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d}\}$ where $\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{j}$ for any $i\geq j$. Let $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}=\mathbf{\tilde{U}}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{\tilde{\Lambda}}^{1/2}&{\mathbf{0}}\end{pmatrix}\mathbf{\tilde{V}}^{\top}$ be the singular value decomposition of $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$, where $\mathbf{\tilde{\Lambda}}:=\diag\{\tilde{\lambda}_{1},\tilde{\lambda}_{2},\ldots,\tilde{\lambda}_{M}\}$ is a diagonal matrix with diagonal entries in non-increasing order. Define $\mathbf{A}_{k}:=\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}$. Then it follows from similar arguments as in Lemma C.1 that $\mathbf{A}_{k}$ is invertible.

Since

\displaystyle\|\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\|_{2}=\gamma_{t}\tilde{\lambda}_{1}\leq\gamma\tilde{\lambda}_{1} \displaystyle\leq\frac{\tilde{\lambda}_{1}}{c\sum_{i=1}^{M}\tilde{\lambda}_{i}}\leq 1

for some constant $c>1$ by the stepsize assumption, it follows that $\mathbf{I}_{M}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\succ{\mathbf{0}}_{M}$ for all $t\in[N]$. Therefore, it can be verified that

\displaystyle\prod_{t=1}^{N}(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\prod_{t=1}^{N}(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})\preceq(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}=:\mathsf{M},

and by definition of $\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})$ in Eq. (5), we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\eqsim\bigg\|\prod_{t=1}^{N}\big(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big){\mathbf{v}^{*}}\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}} \displaystyle\leq\bigg\|\big(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{N_{\mathtt{eff}}}{\mathbf{v}^{*}}\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}} \displaystyle=\langle\mathsf{M},\mathbf{v}^{*\otimes 2}\rangle.

Note that the eigenvalues of $\mathsf{M}$ are $\{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}(1-\gamma\tilde{\lambda}_{i})^{2N_{\mathtt{eff}}}\}_{i=1}^{M}$. Since the function $f(x)=x(1-\gamma x)^{2N_{\mathtt{eff}}}$ is maximized at $x_{0}=1/[(2N_{\mathtt{eff}}+1)\gamma]$ for $x\in[0,1/\gamma]$ with $f(x_{0})\lesssim 1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)$, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{M}\|_{2}\leq c/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)

for some constant $c>0$. The first part of Lemma D.1 follows immediately.

Now we prove the second part of Lemma D.1. Recall that ${\mathbf{v}^{*}}=\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}$. Substituting

\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}&\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\end{pmatrix}

into ${\mathbf{v}^{*}}$, we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mathsf{M},{\mathbf{v}^{*}}^{\otimes 2}\rangle \displaystyle=\langle\mathsf{M},((\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}{\mathbf{w}^{*}})^{\otimes 2}\rangle \displaystyle={\mathbf{w}^{*}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{M}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}{\mathbf{w}^{*}} \displaystyle\leq 2T_{1}+2T_{2},

where

\displaystyle T_{1} \displaystyle:=(\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}})^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{0:k}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{M}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}, \displaystyle T_{2} \displaystyle:=(\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}})^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{M}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}.

We claim the following results which we prove later.

1-e^{-\Omega(M)} \displaystyle T_{1} \displaystyle\leq\frac{c\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}\|_{2}^{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}\cdot\Bigg[\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{A}_{k})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{A}_{k})}\Bigg]^{2} c>0 \displaystyle T_{2} \displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\|_{\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}}^{2}.

Combining Eq. (22a), (22b) gives the second part of Lemma D.1.

Proof of claim (22a)

By definition of $T_{1}$, we have

\displaystyle T_{1} \displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{M}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\|_{2}\cdot\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k}}}\|_{2}^{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\quad\|\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{{0:k}}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{M}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\|_{2} \displaystyle\leq\|\mathsf{M}\|_{2}\cdot\|(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\|_{2}^{2} \displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}\|(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\|_{2}^{2}

for some constant $c>0$, where the last line uses Eq. (19).

It remains to show

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\|_{2}\leq c\cdot\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{A}_{k})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{A}_{k})}

for some constant $c>0$ with probability $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Since $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}=\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}+\mathbf{A}_{k}$, we have

\displaystyle(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k} \displaystyle=(\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}-\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1})\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k} \displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}-\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\mathbf{H}_{0:k} \displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}\mathbf{H}_{0:k} \displaystyle=\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1},

where the second line uses Woodbury’s identity. Since

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\succeq\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}},

it follows that

\displaystyle\|[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\|_{2}\leq\|[\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\|_{2}.

Therefore, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\|_{2} \displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}\cdot\|\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\|_{2}\cdot\|[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\|_{2} \displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}\cdot\|\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\|_{2}\cdot\|[\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\|_{2} \displaystyle\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}\cdot\|\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\|_{2}}{{\mu_{\min}}(\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}})}\lesssim\frac{\|\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}}{{\mu_{\min}}(\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}})}

where the last inequality follows from the fact that $\|\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\|_{2}=\sqrt{\|\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}\|_{2}}\leq c$ for some constant $c>0$ when $k\leq M/2$ with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Since $\mathbf{S}_{0:k}$ is independent of $\mathbf{A}_{k}$ and the distribution of $\mathbf{S}_{0:k}$ is rotationally invariant, we may write $\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{1}{\hat{\lambda}_{M-i}}\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{i}\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{i}^{\top}$, where $\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{i}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(0,\mathbf{I}_{k}/M)$ and $(\hat{\lambda}_{i})_{i=1}^{M}$ are eigenvalues of $\mathbf{A}_{k}$ in non-increasing order. Therefore, for $k\leq M/3$

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{1}{\hat{\lambda}_{M-i}}\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{i}\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{i}^{\top}\succeq\sum_{i=1}^{M/2}\frac{1}{\hat{\lambda}_{M-i}}\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{i}\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{i}^{\top}\succeq\frac{1}{\hat{\lambda}_{M/2}}\sum_{i=1}^{M/2}\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{i}\tilde{\mathbf{s}}_{i}^{\top}\succeq\frac{c\mathbf{I}_{k}}{\hat{\lambda}_{M/2}}

for some constant $c>0$ with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, where in the last line we again use the concentration properties of Gaussian covariance matrices (see e.g., Theorem 6.1 in Wainwright (2019)). As a direct consequence, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}[\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{-1}+\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{{0:k}}]^{-1}\|_{2}\leq c\cdot\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{A}_{k})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{A}_{k})}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ for some constant $c>0.$ This concludes the proof.

Proof of claim (22b)

By definition of $T_{2}$ in Eq. (21), we have

\displaystyle T_{2} \displaystyle={\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1/2}(\mathbf{I}_{M}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{2N_{\mathtt{eff}}}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1/2}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}} \displaystyle\leq{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}^{\top}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}} \displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{k:\infty}\|\cdot\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}} \displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}},

where the last line follows from

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{k:\infty}\|_{2} \displaystyle=\|\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}+\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{k:\infty}\|_{2} \displaystyle\leq\|\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}^{1/2}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}\mathbf{A}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{k:\infty}\|_{2}\leq 1.

∎

Appendix D Bias error > D.2 A lower bound [body]
Lemma D.2 (Lower bound on the bias term).

Suppose $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ follows some prior distribution and the initial stepsize $\gamma\leq\frac{1}{c\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}$ for some constant $c>2$. Let $\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}:={\mathbb{E}}\mathbf{w}^{*}\mathbf{w}^{*\top}$. Then the bias term in Eq. (5) satisfies

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(\gamma N_{\mathtt{eff}})}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}

almost surely.

Proof of Lemma D.2.

Let $\mathsf{M_{N}}:=\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}-2\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{2N_{\mathtt{eff}}}$. Adopt the notations in the proof of Lemma D.1. By definition of the bias term, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\eqsim\bigg\|\prod_{t=1}^{N}\big(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big){\mathbf{v}^{*}}\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}} \displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\prod_{t=1}^{N/N_{\mathtt{eff}}}(\mathbf{I}-\gamma_{N_{\mathtt{eff}}t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{2N_{\mathtt{eff}}},{\mathbf{v}^{*}}^{\otimes 2}\rangle \displaystyle\geq\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}-\sum_{t=1}^{N/N_{\mathtt{eff}}}\gamma_{N_{\mathtt{eff}}t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{2N_{\mathtt{eff}}},{\mathbf{v}^{*}}^{\otimes 2}\rangle \displaystyle\geq\langle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}-2\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{2N_{\mathtt{eff}}},{\mathbf{v}^{*}}^{\otimes 2}\rangle=\langle\mathsf{M_{N}},{\mathbf{v}^{*}}^{\otimes 2}\rangle,

where the third line uses $\mathbf{I}_{M}-2\gamma_{t}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\succ{\mathbf{0}}_{M}$ for all $t\in[N]$ established in the proof of Lemma D.1, $\sum_{i=1}^{N/N_{\mathtt{eff}}}\gamma_{N_{\mathtt{eff}}i}\leq 2\gamma$, and the fact that $(1-w)(1-v)\geq 1-w-v$ for $w,v>0$. Substituting the definition of ${\mathbf{v}^{*}}$ in Eq. (5) into the expression, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\gtrsim{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\langle\mathsf{M_{N}},{\mathbf{v}^{*}}^{\otimes 2}\rangle={\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\langle\mathsf{M_{N}},((\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}{\mathbf{w}^{*}})^{\otimes 2}\rangle \displaystyle=\tr(\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{M_{N}}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}) \displaystyle=\tr((\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{M_{N}}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}) \displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{M}\mu_{M-i+1}((\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathsf{M_{N}}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1})\cdot\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}),

where the last line uses Von Neumann’s trace inequality. Continuing the calculation, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}((\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{2}\mathsf{M_{N}}^{-1})} \displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}\Big((\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})(\mathbf{I}-2\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-2N_{\mathtt{eff}}}\Big)} \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(\gamma N_{\mathtt{eff}})}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})},

where the first inequality uses $\mu_{M+i-1}(A)=\mu^{-1}_{i}(A^{-1})$ for any positive definite matrix $A\in{\mathbb{R}}^{M\times M}$, and the second line follows from the definition of $\mathsf{M_{N}}$ and the fact that $(1-\lambda\gamma N_{\mathtt{eff}})^{-2N_{\mathtt{eff}}}\lesssim 1$ when $\lambda<1/(\gamma N_{\mathtt{eff}})$. ∎

Appendix D Bias error > D.3 Examples on matching bounds for $\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})$ [body]
In this section, we derive matching upper and lower bounds for $\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})$ in (5) in three scenarios: power-law spectrum (Lemma D.3), power-law spectrum with source condition (Lemma D.4) and logarithmic power-law spectrum (Lemma D.5). Recall that we define $\mathsf{Bias}:={\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})$.

Lemma D.3 (Bounds on $\mathsf{Bias}$ under the power-law spectrum).

Suppose Assumption 1C and 2 hold and the initial stepsize $\gamma\leq\frac{1}{c\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}$ for some constant $c>2$. Then with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of $\mathbf{S}$

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\max\big\{(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}-1},\ M^{1-{a}}\big\},

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\gtrsim(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}-1}

when $(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}}\leq M/c$ for some constant $c>0$. Here, all the (hidden) constants depend only on the power-law degree $a.$

Proof of Lemma D.3.

For the upper bound, using Lemma G.5, D.1 and the assumption that ${\mathbb{E}}{\mathbf{w}^{*2}_{i}}=1$ for all $i>0$, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Bigg[\frac{\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k_{2}}}}\|_{2}^{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\Bigg] \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{k_{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+\sum_{k>k_{2}}\lambda_{i} \displaystyle\eqsim\frac{k_{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+k_{2}^{1-a} \displaystyle\lesssim\max\big\{(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}-1},\ M^{1-{a}}\big\},

where in the last inequality, we choose $k_{2}=[M/3]\wedge(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}}$ to minimize the upper bound.

When $(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}}\leq M/3$, combining Lemma D.2 and G.4 gives the lower bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}=\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{2}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}, \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma),i\leq M}\frac{i^{-2a}}{i^{-a}}=\sum_{\lambda_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma),i\leq M}i^{-a}\gtrsim(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}-1}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Here, the hidden constants depend only on $a.$ ∎

Lemma D.4 (Bounds on $\mathsf{Bias}$ under the source condition).

Suppose Assumption 3 hold and the initial stepsize $\gamma\leq\frac{1}{c\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}$ for some constant $c>2$. Then with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of $\mathbf{S}$

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\max\big\{(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{(1-b)/a},\ M^{1-b}\big\},

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\gtrsim(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{(1-b)/a}

when $(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}}\leq M/c$ for some constant $c>0$. Moreover, when $b\geq a+1$, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of $\mathbf{S}$

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\log N_{\mathtt{eff}}\cdot\max\big\{(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{(1-b)/a},\ M^{1-b}\big\}

In all results, the hidden constants depend only on $a,b.$

Proof of Lemma D.4.

For the upper bound, using Lemma G.5, D.1 and the assumption that (w.l.o.g.) ${\mathbb{E}}{\mathbf{w}^{*2}_{i}}\eqsim i^{a-b}$ for all $i>0$, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Bigg[\frac{\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k_{2}}}}\|_{2}^{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\Bigg] \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{k_{2}^{1+a-b}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+\sum_{k>k_{2}}\lambda_{i}\cdot i^{a-b} \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{k_{2}^{1+a-b}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+k_{2}^{1-b} \displaystyle\lesssim\max\big\{(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{(1-b)/a},\ M^{1-b}\big\}

when $b<a+1$, where in the last inequality, we choose $k_{2}=[M/3]\wedge(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}$ to minimize the upper bound.

When $(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}}\leq M/c$ for some large constant $c>0$, combining Lemma D.2 and G.4 yields the lower bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}\eqsim\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{(a+b)/a}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}, \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma),i\leq M}\frac{i^{-(a+b)}}{i^{-a}}=\sum_{\lambda_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma),i\leq M}i^{-b}\gtrsim(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{(1-b)/{a}}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Here, the hidden constants depend only on $a,b.$

Upper bound when $b\geq a+1$.

Following the previous derivations, when $b=a+1$, we have with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Bigg[\frac{\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k_{2}}}}\|_{2}^{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\Bigg] \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\log k_{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+k_{2}^{1-b} \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\log(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+M^{1-b}

where the last line follows by setting $k_{2}=[M/3]\wedge(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}$. When $b>a+1$, we have with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Bigg[\frac{\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k_{2}}}}\|_{2}^{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\Bigg] \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+k_{2}^{1-b} \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+M^{1-b},

where the last follows by chooing $k_{2}=M/3$ to minimize the upper bound.

Note that there exist non-constant gaps between the upper and lower bounds on the bias term (plus the approximation error term, Lemma C.5) in the simple regime where $b\geq a+1$. We leave a more precise analysis of the bias term for future work.

Lemma D.5 (Bounds on $\mathsf{Bias}$ under the logarithmic power-law spectrum).

Suppose Assumption 4 hold and the initial stepsize $\gamma\leq\frac{1}{c\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}$ for some constant $c>2$. Let ${\mathsf{\bar{k}}}:=\inf\{k:k\log^{a}k\geq N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma\}$. Then with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of $\mathbf{S}$

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\lesssim\max\big\{\log^{1-a}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma),\log^{1-{a}}M\big\},

and

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*})\gtrsim\log^{1-a}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)

when $(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)\leq M^{c}$ for some sufficiently small constant $c>0$. Here, all constants depend only on the power-law degree $a.$

Proof of Lemma D.5.

For the upper bound, using Lemma G.7, D.1 and the assumption that ${\mathbb{E}}{\mathbf{w}^{*2}_{i}}=1$ for all $i>0$, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Bigg[\frac{\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{0:k_{2}}}}\|_{2}^{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+\|{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\|^{2}_{\mathbf{H}_{{k_{2}:\infty}}}\Bigg] \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{k_{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+\sum_{k>k_{2}}\lambda_{i} \displaystyle\eqsim\frac{k_{2}}{N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}+\log^{1-a}k_{2} \displaystyle\lesssim\max\big\{\log^{1-{a}}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma),\log^{1-{a}}M\big\},

where in the last inequality, we choose $k_{2}=[M/3]\wedge\big[(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)/\log^{{a}}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)\big]$ to minimize the upper bound.

Recall ${\mathsf{k^{*}}}\eqsim M/\log M$ (for example we may define ${\mathsf{k^{*}}}=\inf\{k:k\log k\geq M\}$) in Lemma G.6. Combining Lemma D.2 and G.6 gives the lower bound

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{H}^{\mathbf{w}}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}\eqsim\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{2}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}, \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma),i\leq{\mathsf{k^{*}}}}\frac{i^{-2}\log^{-2a}i}{i^{-1}\log^{-a}i}=\sum_{\lambda_{i}<1/(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma),i\leq{\mathsf{k^{*}}}}i^{-1}\log^{-a}i \displaystyle\gtrsim\sum_{i=N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma}^{\mathsf{k^{*}}}i^{-1}\log^{-a}i \displaystyle\gtrsim\log^{1-a}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)-\log^{1-a}({\mathsf{k^{*}}}) \displaystyle\gtrsim\log^{1-a}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)-c_{1}\log^{1-a}(M)

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ for some constant $c_{1}>0$. Here, the (hidden) constants depend only on $a.$ Therefore, when $(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/{a}}\leq M^{c}$ for some sufficiently small constant $c>0$, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{{\mathbf{w}^{*}}}\mathsf{Bias}(\mathbf{w}^{*}) \displaystyle\gtrsim\log^{1-a}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)-c_{1}\log^{1-a}(M)\gtrsim\log^{1-a}(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma).

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. ∎

Appendix F Expected risk of the average of (SGD) iterates [body]
In this section, we study the expected risk of the average of (SGD) iterates. Namely, we consider a fixed stepsize (SGD) procedure where $\gamma_{t}=\gamma$ and define $\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{N}:=\sum^{N-1}_{i=0}\mathbf{v}_{i}/N$. Our goal is to derive matching upper and lower bounds $\mathcal{R}(\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{N})$ in terms of the sample size $N$ and model size $M.$ Compared with the last iterate of (SGD) with geometrically decaying stepsizes, we show that the average of (SGD) iterates with a fixed stepsize achieves a better risk, in the sense that the effective sample size $N_{\mathtt{eff}}$ is replaced by $N$ in the bounds (c.f. We start with invoking the following result in Zou et al. (2023).

Theorem F.1 (A variant of Theorem 2.1 and 2.2 in (Zou et al., 2023)).

Conditional on $\mathbf{S}$ and suppose the stepsize $\gamma<1/(c\tr(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}))$ for some constant $c>0$, then there exist $\mathsf{Approx},\mathsf{Bias},\mathsf{Var}$ such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{R}_{M}({\mathbf{\bar{v}}}_{N})-\sigma^{2}\eqsim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\mathsf{Approx}+\mathsf{Bias}+\sigma^{2}\mathsf{Var},

where the expectation of $\mathcal{R}_{M}$ is over $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ and $(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{N}$ and

\displaystyle\mathsf{Approx} \displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\xi^{2} \displaystyle=\Big\|\Big(\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big)\mathbf{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}{\mathbf{w}^{*}}\Big\|^{2},

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}({T}_{1}+{T}_{3}) \displaystyle\lesssim\mathsf{Bias}\lesssim{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}({T}_{2}+{T}_{4}), \displaystyle\mathsf{Var} \displaystyle\eqsim\frac{{D_{\mathrm{eff},N}}}{N},

and

\displaystyle{T}_{1} \displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}N^{2}}\tr\Big(\Big(\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{N/4}\Big)^{2}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{0}\Big), \displaystyle{T}_{2} \displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}N^{2}}\tr\Big(\Big(\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{N}\Big)^{2}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{-1}\mathbf{B}_{0}\Big), \displaystyle{T}_{3} \displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\gamma N^{2}}\tr\Big(\Big(\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{N/4}\Big)\mathbf{B}_{0}\Big)\cdot\tr\Big(\big(\mathbf{I}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{N/4}\big)^{2}\Big), \displaystyle{T}_{4} \displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\gamma N}\tr\big(\mathbf{B}_{0}-(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{N}\mathbf{B}_{0}(\mathbf{I}-\gamma\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})^{N}\big)\cdot\frac{{D_{\mathrm{eff},N}}}{N}, \displaystyle\mathbf{B}_{0} \displaystyle:={\mathbf{v}^{*}}{\mathbf{v}^{*}}^{\top}, \displaystyle{D_{\mathrm{eff},N}} \displaystyle:=\#\{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\geq 1/(N\gamma)\}+(N\gamma)^{2}\sum_{\tilde{\lambda}_{j}<1/(N\gamma)}\tilde{\lambda}_{j}^{2}, (\tilde{\lambda}_{j})_{j=1}^{M} \mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}

See Section F.2.1 for the proof.

For $T_{i}(i=1,2,3,4)$, we also have the following upper (and lower) bounds.

Lemma F.2 (Lower bound on ${T}_{1}$).

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{\mathbf{w}^{*}}T_{1}\gtrsim\sum_{i:\tilde{\lambda}_{i}<1/(\gamma N)}\frac{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{2}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}{\mu_{i}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})}

Appendix F Expected risk of the average of (SGD) iterates > F.1 Matching bounds for the average of (SGD) iterates under power-law spectrum [body]
In this section, we derive upper and lower bounds for the expected risk under the power-law spectrum. Our main result (Theorem F.6) follows directly from Theorem F.1 and the bounds on ${T}_{i}(i=1,2,3,4)$ in Lemmas F.2, F.3, F.4 and F.5.

Theorem F.6 (Scaling law for average iterates of SGD).

Suppose Assumption 1 and 2 hold and $\sigma^{2}\lesssim 1$. Then there exists some $a$-dependent constant $c>0$ such that when $\gamma\leq c$, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ over the randomness of the sketch matrix $\mathbf{S}$, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathcal{R}_{M}(\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{N})=\sigma^{2}+\Theta\big(M^{1-a}\big)+\Theta\big((N\gamma)^{1/a-1}\big),

where the expectation is over the randomness of $\mathbf{w}^{*}$ and $(\mathbf{x}_{i},y_{i})_{i=1}^{N}$, and $\Theta(\cdot)$ hides constants that may depend on $a$.

See the proof in Section F.2.6.

Compared with Theorem 4.1, Theorem F.6 suggests that the average of (SGD) achieves a smaller risk in the sketched linear model—the $(N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma)^{1/a}$ is replaced by $(N\gamma)^{1/a}$ in the bound for the bias term. This is intuitive since the sum of stepsizes $\sum_{t}\gamma_{t}\eqsim N_{\mathtt{eff}}\gamma$ for the geometrically decaying stepsize scheduler while $\sum_{t}\gamma_{t}\eqsim N\gamma$ for the fixed stepsize scheduler.

We also verify the observations in Theorem F.6 via simulations. We adopt the same model and setup as in Section 5 but use the average of iterates of fixed stepsize (SGD) (denoted by $\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{N}$) as the predictor. From Figure 3 and 4 we see that the expected risk ${\mathbb{E}}\mathcal{R}(\bar{\mathbf{v}}_{N})$ also scales following a power-law relation in both sample size $N$ and model size $M$. Moreover, the fitted exponents match our theoretical predictions in Theorem F.6.

Lemma G.1.

Suppose that $\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times d}$ is such that

Footnote: 3 3 We allow $d=\infty$.

\mathbf{S}_{ij}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1/M).

Let $(\lambda_{i})_{i\geq 1}$ be the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ in non-increasing order. Let $(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})_{i=1}^{M}$ be the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$ in non-increasing order. Then there exists a constant $c>1$ such that for every $M\geq 0$ and every $0\leq k\leq M$, with probability $\geq 1-e^{-\Omega(M)}-e^{-\Omega(k)}$, we have

\displaystyle\text{for every}\ j\leq M,\quad\bigg|\tilde{\lambda}_{j}-\bigg(\lambda_{j}+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\bigg)\bigg|\leq c\cdot\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Bigg).

As a direct consequence, for $k\leq M/c^{2}$, we have

\displaystyle\text{for every}\ j\leq M,\quad\bigg|\tilde{\lambda}_{j}-\bigg(\lambda_{j}+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\bigg)\bigg|\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\bigg(\lambda_{j}+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\bigg)+c_{1}\cdot\lambda_{k+1},

where $c_{1}=c+2c^{2}$.

Proof of Lemma G.1.

We have the following decomposition motivated by Swartworth and Woodruff (2023) (see their Section 3.4, Proof of Theorem 1).

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top} \displaystyle=\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}+\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top} \displaystyle=\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}+\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}-\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}.

We remark that this decomposition idea has been implicitly used in Bartlett et al. (2020) to control the eigenvalues of a Gram matrix. In fact, we will use techniques from Bartlett et al. (2020) to obtain a sharper bound than that presented in Swartworth and Woodruff (2023).

For the upper bound, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big) \displaystyle\leq\mu_{j}\bigg(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg)+\bigg\|\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}-\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg\|_{2} \displaystyle=\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}+\bigg\|\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}-\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg\|_{2} \displaystyle\leq\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}+c_{1}\cdot\Bigg(\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Bigg),

where the last inequality is by Lemma G.2. For $j\leq k$, using Lemma G.3, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)\leq\lambda_{j}+c_{2}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j}.

For $k<j\leq M$, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)=0\leq\lambda_{j}+c_{2}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j}.

Putting these together, we have the following for every $j=1,\dots,M$:

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big) \displaystyle\leq\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}+c_{1}\cdot\Bigg(\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Bigg) \displaystyle\leq\lambda_{j}+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}+c\cdot\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Bigg).

Similarly, we can show the lower bound. By the decomposition, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big) \displaystyle\geq\mu_{j}\bigg(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg)-\bigg\|\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}-\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg\| \displaystyle=\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}-\bigg\|\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}-\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg\| \displaystyle\geq\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}-c_{1}\cdot\Bigg(\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Bigg),

where the last inequality is by Lemma G.2. For $j\leq k$, using Lemma G.3, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)\geq\lambda_{j}-c_{2}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j}.

For $k<j\leq M$, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)=0\geq\lambda_{j}-\lambda_{k+1}-c_{2}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j},

where the last inequality is due to $\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda_{k}$ for $j\geq k$. Putting these together, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}\big) \displaystyle\geq\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\big)+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}-c_{1}\cdot\Bigg(\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Bigg) \displaystyle\geq\lambda_{j}+\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}-c\cdot\Bigg(\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j}+\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}\Bigg).

So far, we have proved the first claim. To show the second claim, we simply apply

c\cdot\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{for}\ k\leq M/c^{2},

and

\displaystyle c\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}} \displaystyle\leq c\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\lambda_{k+1}} \displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}+2c^{2}\cdot\lambda_{k+1},

in the first claim. ∎

Lemma G.2 (Tail concentration, Lemma 26 in Bartlett et al. (2020)).

For any $k\geq 0$, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}-\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg\|_{2}\lesssim\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}}.

Moreover, the minimum eigenvalue of $\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}$ satisfies

\displaystyle{\mu_{\min}}(\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top})\gtrsim\lambda_{k+2M}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$.

Proof of Lemma G.2.

The first part of Lemma G.2 is a version of Lemma 26 in (Bartlett et al., 2020) (see their proof). We provide proof here for completeness.

We write $\mathbf{S}\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times p}$ as

\mathbf{S}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{s}_{1}&\ldots&\mathbf{s}_{p}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{s}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\ \frac{1}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg),\quad i\geq 1.

Since Gaussian distribution is rotational invariance, without loss of generality, we may assume

\mathbf{H}=\diag\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{p}\}.

Then we have

\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}=\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\top}.

Fixing a unit vector $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}$, then

\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}\big(\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{v}\big)^{2},

where each $\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{v}$ is $(1/M)$-subGaussian. By Bernstein’s inequality, we have, with probability $\geq 1-\delta$,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}\big(\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{v}\big)^{2}-\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\bigg|\lesssim\frac{1}{M}\cdot\bigg(\lambda_{k+1}\cdot\log\frac{1}{\delta}+\sqrt{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}\cdot\log\frac{1}{\delta}}\bigg).

By a union bound and net argument on $\mathcal{S}^{M-1}$, we have, with probability $\geq 1-\delta$, for every unit vector $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{M}$,

\displaystyle\bigg|\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}\big(\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{v}\big)^{2}-\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\bigg|\lesssim\frac{1}{M}\cdot\Bigg(\lambda_{k+1}\cdot\bigg(M+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\bigg)+\sqrt{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}\cdot\bigg(M+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\bigg)}\Bigg).

So with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, we have

\displaystyle\bigg\|\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}-\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}\bigg\|_{2} \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{M}\cdot\Bigg(\lambda_{k+1}\cdot M+\sqrt{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}\cdot M}\Bigg) \displaystyle\eqsim\lambda_{k+1}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}}{M}},

which completes the proof of the first part of Lemma G.2.

To prove the second part of Lemma G.2, it suffices to note that

\displaystyle\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}\succeq\sum_{i=k+1}^{2M+k}\lambda_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\top}\succeq\lambda_{2M+k}\cdot\sum_{i=k+1}^{2M+k}\mathbf{s}_{i}\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\top}\succeq c\lambda_{2M+k}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{M}

for some constant $c>1$ with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, where the last line follows from concentration properties of Gaussian covariance matrices (see e.g., Thereom 6.1 Wainwright (2019)). ∎

Lemma G.3 (Head concentration).

With probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}-e^{-\Omega(k)}$, we have

\displaystyle\text{for every}\ j\leq k,\quad|\mu_{j}(\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top})-\lambda_{j}|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j}.

Proof of Lemma G.3.

Note that the spectrum of $\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}$ is indentical to the spectrum of $\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{0:k}$. We will bound the latter. We start with bounding the spectrum of $\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}$. To this end, we write $\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times M}$ as

\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{0:k}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{s}_{1}&\ldots&\mathbf{s}_{M}\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathbf{s}_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}\bigg(0,\ \frac{1}{M}\cdot\mathbf{I}_{k}\bigg),\quad i=1,\dots,M.

Then repeating the argument in Lemma G.2, we have, with probability $\geq 1-\delta$, for every unit vector $\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}$,

\displaystyle\big|\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{v}-1\big| \displaystyle=\bigg|\sum_{i=1}^{M}\big(\mathbf{s}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{v}\big)^{2}-1\bigg| \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{M}\cdot\Bigg(1\cdot\bigg(k+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\bigg)+\sqrt{M\cdot\bigg(k+\log\frac{1}{\delta}\bigg)}\Bigg) \displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{k+\log(1/\delta)}{M}}.

So we have, with probability $\geq 1-e^{-\Omega(k)}$,

\displaystyle\big\|\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}-\mathbf{I}_{k}\big\|_{2}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{0:k}\big) \displaystyle\leq\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\big)+c_{1}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\big) \displaystyle=\lambda_{j}+c_{1}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j},

and that

\displaystyle\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{0:k}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}^{\top}\mathbf{S}_{0:k}\mathbf{H}^{1/2}_{0:k}\big) \displaystyle\geq\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\big)-c_{1}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\mu_{j}\big(\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\mathbf{H}_{0:k}^{1/2}\big) \displaystyle=\lambda_{j}-c_{1}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{k}{M}}\cdot\lambda_{j}.

We have completed the proof. ∎

Lemma G.4 (Eigenvalues of $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$ under power-law spectrum).

Suppose Assumption 2 hold. There exist $a$-dependent constants $c_{2}>c_{1}>0$ such that

\displaystyle c_{1}j^{-{a}}\leq\mu_{j}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})\leq c_{2}j^{-{a}}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}.$

Proof of Lemma G.4.

Let $(\tilde{\lambda}_{i})_{i=1}^{M}$ denote the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$ in non-increasing order. Using Lemma G.1 with $k=M/c$ for some sufficiently large constant $c$ and noting that $\sum_{i>k}i^{-{a}}\eqsim k^{1-{a}}$, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cdot(j^{-{a}}+\tilde{c}_{1}M^{-{a}})-\tilde{c}_{2}\cdot M^{-{a}}\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\leq\frac{3}{2}\cdot(j^{-{a}}+\tilde{c}_{1}M^{-{a}})+\tilde{c}_{2}\cdot M^{-{a}}

for every $j\in[M]$ for some constants $\tilde{c}_{i},i\in[2]$ with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Therefore, for all $j\leq M/\tilde{c}$ for some sufficiently large constant $\tilde{c}>1$, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\in[\tilde{c}_{3}j^{-{a}},\tilde{c}_{4}j^{-{a}}]

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ for some constants $\tilde{c}_{3},\tilde{c}_{4}>0$. For $j\in[M/\tilde{c},M]$, by monotonicity of the eigenvalues, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{\lfloor M/\tilde{c}\rfloor}\leq\tilde{c}_{4}\Big(\Big\lfloor\frac{M}{\tilde{c}}\Big\rfloor\Big)^{-{a}}\leq\tilde{c}_{5}M^{-{a}}\leq\tilde{c}_{5}j^{-{a}}

for some sufficiently large constant $\tilde{c}_{5}>\tilde{c}_{4}$ with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Moreover, using Lemma G.2 with $k=0$, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\geq\tilde{\lambda}_{M}\geq{\mu_{\min}}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top})\geq\tilde{c}_{6}\tilde{\lambda}_{2M}\geq\tilde{c}_{7}(M/\tilde{c})^{-{a}}\geq\tilde{c}_{8}j^{-{a}}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ for some constants $\tilde{c}_{6},\tilde{c}_{7},\tilde{c}_{8}>0$. Combining the bounds for $j\leq M/\tilde{c}$ and $j\in[M/\tilde{c},M]$ completes the proof. ∎

Lemma G.5 (Ratio of eigenvalues of $\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}$ under power-law spectrum).

Suppose Assumption 2 hold. There exists some $a$-dependent constant $c>0$ such that for any $k\geq 1$, the ratio between the $M/2$-th and $M$-th eigenvalues

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}\leq c

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}.$

Proof of Lemma G.5.

We prove the lemma under two scenarios where $k$ is relatively small (or large) compared with $M$.

Let $c>0$ be some sufficiently large constant. Applying Lemma G.1 with $\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}$ replacing $\mathbf{H}$, for $k_{0}=M/c$, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\cdot\bigg(\lambda_{M/2+k}+\frac{\sum_{i>k_{0}}\lambda_{i+k}}{M}\bigg)+c_{1}\cdot\lambda_{k_{0}+1+k}, \displaystyle\lesssim\big(\frac{M}{2}+k\big)^{-{a}}+\frac{(k_{0}+k)^{1-{a}}}{M}+(k_{0}+1+k)^{-{a}} \displaystyle\lesssim(k\vee M)^{-{a}}+(k\vee M)^{-{a}}\big(1\vee\frac{k}{M}\big)+(k\vee M)^{-{a}} \displaystyle\lesssim(k\vee M)^{-{a}}\big(1\vee\frac{k}{M}\big)

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ for some constant $c_{1}>0$.

Case $1$: $k\lesssim M$

From Lemma G.2, we have

\displaystyle{\mu_{\min}}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\gtrsim\lambda_{k+2M}\gtrsim(k\vee M)^{-{a}}.

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}.$ Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}\lesssim 1

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ when $k/M\lesssim 1$.

Case $2$: $k\gtrsim M$

On the other hand, when $k$ is relatively large, using Lemma G.1 with $\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}$ replacing $\mathbf{H}$ again, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\bigg(\lambda_{M+k}+\frac{\sum_{i>k_{0}}\lambda_{i+k}}{M}\bigg)-c_{1}\cdot\lambda_{k_{0}+1+k}, \displaystyle\geq c_{2}\Big[\big({M}+k\big)^{-{a}}+\frac{(k_{0}+k)^{1-{a}}}{M}\Big]-c_{3}\cdot(k_{0}+1+k)^{-{a}}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}-e^{-\Omega(k_{0})}$, where $c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0$ are some universal constants. Choosing $k_{0}=M/c^{2}$ for some sufficiently large constant $c>0$, we further obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\geq c_{4}\big({M}+k\big)^{-{a}}\Big[1+\frac{k}{M}\Big]-c_{5}(M+k)^{-{a}} \displaystyle\geq c_{6}\big({M}\vee k\big)^{-{a}}\Big[1\vee\frac{k}{M}\Big]-c_{7}(M\vee k)^{-{a}}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, where $(c_{i})_{i=4}^{7}$ are $a$-dependent constants. Since

\displaystyle c_{6}\big({M}\vee k\big)^{-{a}}\Big[1\vee\frac{k}{M}\Big]-c_{7}(M\vee k)^{-{a}}\geq\frac{c_{6}}{2}(k\vee M)^{-{a}}\big(1\vee\frac{k}{M}\big)

when $k$ is large, i.e., $k/M>\tilde{c}$ for some sufficiently large $a$-dependent constant $\tilde{c}>0$ that may depend on $(c_{i})_{i=1}^{7}$, we have from Eq. (35) and (36) that

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}\lesssim\frac{(k\vee M)^{-{a}}\big(1\vee\frac{k}{M}\big)}{(k\vee M)^{-{a}}\big(1\vee\frac{k}{M}\big)}\lesssim 1

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. ∎

Appendix G Concentration lemmas > G.3 Concentration results under logarithmic power-law spectrum [body]
Lemma G.6 (Theorem 6 in (Bartlett et al., 2020)).

Suppose Assumption 4 hold. Then there exist some $a$-dependent constants $c,\tilde{c}>0$ such that, with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$

\displaystyle\mu_{j}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{S}^{\top})\in\begin{cases}[c\cdot j^{-1}\log^{-a}(j+1),\tilde{c}\cdot j^{-1}\log^{-a}(j+1)]&j\leq{\mathsf{k^{*}}},\\ [c\cdot M^{-1}\log^{1-a}(M),\tilde{c}\cdot M^{-1}\log^{1-a}(M)]&{\mathsf{k^{*}}}<j\leq M,\end{cases}

where ${\mathsf{k^{*}}}\eqsim M/\log(M)$. Also, there exists some $a$-dependent constants $c_{1},c_{2}>0$ such that

\displaystyle\frac{c_{1}}{j\log^{2a}(j+1)}\leq\mu_{j}(\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{2}\mathbf{S}^{\top})\leq\frac{c_{2}}{j\log^{2a}(j+1)}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$.

Proof of Lemma G.6.

The proof is adapted from the proof of Theorem 6 in (Bartlett et al., 2020). We include it here for completeness.

First part of Lemma G.6.

In Lemma G.1, for some constant $c>1$, choose

{\mathsf{k^{*}}}:=\min\bigg\{k\geq 0:\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}\geq c\cdot M\cdot\lambda_{k+1}\bigg\}.

Then with probability $\geq 1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, we have:

\displaystyle\text{for every $1\leq j\leq M$},\quad\frac{1}{c_{1}}\cdot\bigg(\lambda_{j}+\frac{\sum_{i>{\mathsf{k^{*}}}}\lambda_{i}}{M}\bigg)\leq\tilde{\lambda}_{j}\leq c_{1}\cdot\bigg(\lambda_{j}+\frac{\sum_{i>{\mathsf{k^{*}}}}\lambda_{i}}{M}\bigg),

where $c_{1}>1$ is a constant.

When $\lambda_{j}\eqsim j^{-1}\log^{-a}(j+1)$, we have

{\mathsf{k^{*}}}\eqsim M/\log(M),

and

\displaystyle\sum_{i>{\mathsf{k^{*}}}}\lambda_{i}\eqsim\log^{1-a}({\mathsf{k^{*}}})\eqsim\log^{1-a}(M).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{j} \displaystyle\eqsim\lambda_{j}+\frac{\sum_{i>{\mathsf{k^{*}}}}\lambda_{i}}{M} \displaystyle\eqsim\begin{cases}j^{-1}\log^{-a}(j+1)&j\leq{\mathsf{k^{*}}},\\ M^{-1}\log^{1-a}(M)&{\mathsf{k^{*}}}<j\leq M,\end{cases}

where ${\mathsf{k^{*}}}\eqsim M/\log(M)$.

Second part of Lemma G.6.

Let $\bar{\lambda}_{i}$ denote the $i$-th eigenvalue of $\mathbf{S}\mathbf{H}^{2}\mathbf{S}^{\top}$ for $i\in[M]$. Using Lemma G.1 with $k=M/c$ for some sufficiently large constant $c_{0}$ and noting that $\sum_{i>k}\lambda_{i}^{2}\eqsim\sum_{i>k}i^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}(i+1)\lesssim k^{-1}\log^{-2{a}}k$, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\cdot j^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}(j+1)-\tilde{c}_{2}\cdot M^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}M \displaystyle\qquad\leq\bar{\lambda}_{j}\leq\frac{3}{2}\cdot(j^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}(j+1)+\tilde{c}_{1}M^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}M)+\tilde{c}_{2}\cdot M^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}M

for every $j\in[M]$ for some constants $\tilde{c}_{i},i\in[2]$ with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Therefore, for all $j\leq M/\tilde{c}$ for some sufficiently large constant $\tilde{c}>1$, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}_{j}\in[\tilde{c}_{3}\cdot j^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}(j+1),\tilde{c}_{4}\cdot j^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}(j+1)]

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ for some constants $\tilde{c}_{3},\tilde{c}_{4}>0$. For $j\in[M/\tilde{c},M]$, by monotonicity of the eigenvalues, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}_{j}\leq\bar{\lambda}_{\lfloor M/\tilde{c}\rfloor}\leq\tilde{c}_{4}\Big(\Big\lfloor\frac{M}{\tilde{c}}\Big\rfloor\Big)^{-2}\log^{-2a}\Big(\Big\lfloor\frac{M}{\tilde{c}}\Big\rfloor\Big)\leq\tilde{c}_{5}M^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}M\leq\tilde{c}_{6}\cdot j^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}(j+1)

for some constants $c_{5},c_{6}>0$ with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Moreover, using Lemma G.2 with $k=0$, we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}_{j}\geq\bar{\lambda}_{M}\geq{\mu_{\min}}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}^{2}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top})\geq\tilde{c}_{7}\bar{\lambda}_{2M}\geq\tilde{c}_{8}\cdot j^{-2}\log^{-2{a}}(j+1)

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ for some constants $\tilde{c}_{7},\tilde{c}_{8}>0$ when $j\in[M/\tilde{c},M]$. Combining the bounds for $j\leq M/\tilde{c}$ and $j\in[M/\tilde{c},M]$ completes the proof. ∎

Lemma G.7 (Ratio of eigenvalues of $\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}_{k:\infty}^{\top}$ under logarithmic power-law spectrum).

Suppose Assumption 4 hold. There exists some $a$-dependent constant $c>0$ such that for any $k\geq 1$, the ratio between the $M/2$-th and $M$-th eigenvalues

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}\leq c

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}.$

Proof of Lemma G.7.

Similar to the proof of Lemma G.5, we prove the lemma under two scenarios where $k$ is relatively small (or large) compared with $M$.

Let $c>0$ be some sufficiently large constant. Applying Lemma G.1 with $\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}$ replacing $\mathbf{H}$, for $k_{0}=M/c$, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\cdot\bigg(\lambda_{M/2+k}+\frac{\sum_{i>k_{0}}\lambda_{i+k}}{M}\bigg)+c_{1}\cdot\lambda_{k_{0}+1+k}, \displaystyle\lesssim\big(\frac{M}{2}+k\big)^{-1}\log^{-a}\big(\frac{M}{2}+k\big)+\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(k_{0}+k)}{M}+\frac{\log^{-{a}}(k_{0}+1+k)}{k_{0}+1+k} \displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\log^{-a}(M+k)}{(M+k)}+\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(M+k)}{M}\lesssim\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(M+k)}{M}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ for some constant $c_{1}>0$.

Case $1$: $k\lesssim M$.

Applying Lemma G.1 with $\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}$ replacing $\mathbf{H}$, for $k_{0}=M/c$, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{1}{2}\cdot\bigg(\lambda_{M+k}+\frac{\sum_{i>k_{0}}\lambda_{i+k}}{M}\bigg)-c_{1}\cdot\lambda_{k_{0}+1+k}, \displaystyle\gtrsim\big({M}+k\big)^{-1}\log^{-a}\big({M}+k\big)+\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(k_{0}+k)}{M}-c\frac{\log^{-{a}}(k_{0}+1+k)}{k_{0}+1+k} \displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{\log^{-a}(M+k)}{(M+k)}+\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(M+k)}{M}-\tilde{c}\frac{\log^{-{a}}(M)}{M} \displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{\log^{1-{a}}M}{M}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}\lesssim\Big[\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(M+k)}{M}\Big]\Big/\Big[\frac{\log^{1-{a}}M}{M}\Big]\lesssim 1

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ when $k/M\lesssim 1$.

Case $2$: $k\gtrsim M$.

On the other hand, when $k$ is relatively large, using Lemma G.1 with $\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}$ replacing $\mathbf{H}$ and $k_{0}=M/c$ again, we obtain

\displaystyle\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty}) \displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\cdot\bigg(\lambda_{M+k}+\frac{\sum_{i>k_{0}}\lambda_{i+k}}{M}\bigg)-c_{1}\cdot\lambda_{k_{0}+1+k}, \displaystyle\gtrsim\big({M}+k\big)^{-1}\log^{-a}\big({M}+k\big)+\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(k_{0}+k)}{M}-c_{2}\frac{\log^{-{a}}(k_{0}+1+k)}{k_{0}+1+k} \displaystyle\gtrsim k^{-1}\log^{-a}\big(k\big)+\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(M+k)}{M}-c_{3}\frac{\log^{-{a}}(M+k)}{M+k} \displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(M+k)}{M}

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$, where $c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}>0$ are some $a$-dependent constants. Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{M/2}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}{\mu_{M}(\mathbf{S}_{{k:\infty}}\mathbf{H}_{k:\infty}\mathbf{S}^{\top}_{k:\infty})}\lesssim\Big[\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(M+k)}{M}\Big]\Big/\Big[\frac{\log^{1-{a}}(M+k)}{M}\Big]\lesssim 1

with probability at least $1-e^{-\Omega(M)}$ when $k/M\gtrsim 1$. ∎
